Chinese tax for a small company is not complicated, but the rates depend on two classifications that founders often get wrong: small-scale versus general VAT taxpayer, and small low-profit versus ordinary enterprise for corporate income tax.

Both classifications are worth understanding before the first invoice, because they change the price you quote and the records you keep.

## VAT: the two taxpayer statuses

| | Small-scale taxpayer (小规模纳税人) | General taxpayer (一般纳税人) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rate | 1% on sales (reduced from 3%, through 31 Dec 2027) | 13% goods, 9% transport/construction/leasing, 6% services |
| Input VAT credit | No | Yes, against valid special fapiao |
| Threshold to stay | Annual sales up to ¥5 million | Can elect earlier; mandatory above ¥5 million |
| Accounting burden | Light | Heavier: invoices, input matching, monthly filing |

A consulting company billing ¥1 million a year as a small-scale taxpayer pays about ¥10,000 of VAT and cannot credit its costs. The same company as a general taxpayer bills 6% (¥60,000) but credits input VAT on rent, software, travel and subcontractors.

Which is better depends on the customer. Corporate customers usually want a **special VAT fapiao (专用发票)** so they can credit the input. A small-scale taxpayer can issue one, but at 1% — worth less to the customer than a 6% invoice.

:::warn
The 1% small-scale rate and the ¥100,000 monthly VAT exemption are policy measures legislated through **31 December 2027**. Assume they can change after that, and do not build a five-year price list on them.
:::

## The small-scale exemption

Small-scale taxpayers with monthly sales of **¥100,000 or less** (¥300,000 per quarter) are exempt from VAT. Above the threshold, the whole amount is taxed, not just the excess.

Plan invoicing around the threshold: a single large invoice in a quarter can push the company over and make the entire quarter taxable.

## Corporate income tax

The standard rate is **25%** of taxable profit.

Small low-profit enterprises (小型微利企业) pay an effective **5%**. To qualify, the company must meet all three tests for the year:

- Annual taxable income not exceeding **¥3 million**.
- Fewer than **300 employees**.
- Total assets not exceeding **¥50 million**.

The 5% is not a separate rate: taxable income is reduced and then taxed at 20%, producing roughly 5% effective. The policy runs through **31 December 2027**.

For a founder-run company with ¥1 million of profit, the difference between 25% and 5% is ¥200,000 — one of the largest single tax decisions in the business.

## Surcharges

Surcharges are levied on the VAT actually paid:

| Surcharge | Rate | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Urban maintenance and construction tax | 7% (city), 5% (county), 1% (other) | Based on location |
| Education surcharge | 3% | Nationwide |
| Local education surcharge | 2% | Nationwide |

So roughly 12% of VAT in a city. Small-scale taxpayers and small low-profit enterprises get a **50% reduction** on these and several other small taxes (the "six taxes and two fees" relief), through 31 December 2027.

## Stamp duty

Stamp duty is small but easy to forget, and it is assessed on the contract, not the profit:

| Document | Rate |
| --- | --- |
| Purchase and sale contract | 0.03% |
| Lease contract | 0.1% |
| Equity transfer document | 0.05% |
| Business account books (paid-in capital plus capital reserve) | 0.025% |

Small-scale taxpayers pay half. On a ¥1 million lease that is ¥500–1,000 — not a planning problem, but an audit item if unfiled.

## Payroll taxes and social insurance

Salaries trigger individual income tax (IIT) withholding at progressive rates of **3–45%**, plus employer social insurance and housing fund contributions of roughly **25–32%** of gross salary depending on the city.

The company withholds and remits IIT monthly, and files the annual reconciliation. For a foreign employee, the tax position depends on residence days and treaty tie-breakers — see [the work permit guide](/guides/work-permit-for-foreign-founders/).

## Withholding tax on payments abroad

Payments to a foreign company — dividends, royalties, service fees, interest — are subject to **10% withholding tax** unless a treaty reduces it, plus VAT at 6% on services and royalties. The payer withholds and remits.

Treaty relief requires a certificate of residence and, for dividends, evidence of beneficial ownership and the holding percentage.

## What the first year looks like

1. **Register with the tax bureau** as part of business registration; choose small-scale or general taxpayer status.
2. **File VAT** monthly or quarterly, depending on status.
3. **Withhold IIT** monthly and remit social insurance.
4. **Prepay corporate income tax** quarterly.
5. **File the annual CIT reconciliation** by **31 May** of the following year.
6. **File the annual report** with the market regulator by **30 June**.

Late filing triggers penalties and, worse, a tax credit rating downgrade that affects fapiao limits and bank relationships.

:::note
The cheapest tax planning for a small company is bookkeeping discipline: every cost invoice in the company's name, every contract stamped, every payment through the company account. A founder who mixes personal and company spending pays more tax and loses the audit.
:::

## Two classifications, one decision each

- **VAT status**: small-scale if your customers are consumers or small businesses; general if you sell to corporates that need special fapiao and you have creditable input.
- **CIT status**: keep taxable income under ¥3 million, employees under 300 and assets under ¥50 million to stay at the 5% effective rate. If you are near a threshold, the timing of revenue and expenses in December matters.

:::checklist
- VAT status chosen against the customer base, not the accountant's convenience
- Monthly or quarterly sales monitored against the ¥100,000 exemption threshold
- Small low-profit tests checked before year end (income, headcount, assets)
- All cost invoices issued in the company's name
- Stamp duty identified on leases, sales and equity documents
- Withholding tax assessed before any payment abroad
- Annual CIT reconciliation and annual report dates in the calendar
:::

:::rule
The two classifications — small-scale or general VAT taxpayer, small low-profit or ordinary CIT — decide most of the bill. Choose them deliberately, review them yearly, and assume the reliefs expire in 2027.
:::
