Chinese company law offers several vehicles to a foreign investor. In practice, three account for almost all foreign-founded companies: the wholly foreign-owned enterprise (WFOE), the equity joint venture (JV) and the representative office (RO). A fourth, the foreign-invested partnership, exists and is occasionally useful.

The choice is usually decided by two questions — what the business is allowed to do, and who controls it — long before tax enters the room.

## The WFOE

A WFOE is a limited liability company owned by one or more foreign shareholders. Since the Foreign Investment Law took effect in 2020 it is governed by the Company Law, with a small overlay of foreign-investment rules.

**What it can do:** trade within its business scope, issue fapiao, employ staff, hold licences, own assets, remit profits. In an open sector it can be 100% foreign-owned.

**What it cannot do:** anything outside its registered business scope, anything requiring a licence it does not hold, and — for most founders — borrow freely from abroad without foreign-debt registration.

**Governance:** a shareholder (or shareholders), an executive director or board, a supervisor, a legal representative. The company seal is a real instrument of control; whoever holds it can bind the company.

## The joint venture

A JV is a limited liability company with at least one Chinese and one foreign shareholder. It is still the required or practical vehicle in parts of education, healthcare, telecoms and finance, and it is sometimes the only way to obtain land, a licence or a distribution network.

**What it buys you:** access to a restricted sector, local relationships, a partner who knows the regulator, and sometimes capital.

**What it costs you:** control. Deadlocks, related-party transactions, and a partner who understands the business better than you do are the standard failure modes. Most JV disputes are not about the law; they are about who decides.

:::warn
A JV agreement can allocate board seats, veto rights, dividends and exit rights, but it cannot make an incompatible partner compatible. Before signing, ask what happens on day 400 if the partner stops cooperating — and write that answer into the contract.
:::

## The representative office

An RO is not a company. It is a liaison office of a foreign company, registered with the market regulator and able to conduct "contact, market research and liaison" activities. It cannot sign contracts in its own name for business, cannot issue fapiao, cannot receive business revenue, and cannot employ Chinese staff directly in most cities — staff are usually hired through a service agency.

It is cheap and simple, and for a sourcing or quality-control presence it is often exactly right. It is the wrong vehicle for anything that needs to invoice.

## Comparing the three

| | WFOE | JV | RO |
|---|---|---|---|
| Foreign ownership | 100% in open sectors | Shared | N/A |
| Can invoice customers | Yes | Yes | No |
| Can employ staff directly | Yes | Yes | Usually via agency |
| Minimum capital | No general minimum | Negotiated | None, but funding proof |
| Setup time (typical) | 3–8 weeks | 2–5 months | 3–6 weeks |
| Annual compliance cost | ¥15,000–¥40,000 | Higher, plus audit | ¥10,000–¥25,000 |
| Liability | Limited to subscribed capital | Limited | Foreign parent liable |
| Exit | Deregistration or sale | Negotiated | Closure filing |

Figures are 2026 ranges for a small service business in a tier-one city. They exclude rent and salaries.

## The control questions

Before comparing tax, answer these:

1. **Who holds the seal and the bank U-key?** In a WFOE, you do. In a JV, this is a negotiation, and it matters more than the equity split.
2. **Who is the legal representative?** That person can be personally restricted if the company breaches obligations. Founders often take the role; some regret it.
3. **Who appoints the supervisor?** The supervisor can inspect books and must sign off on related-party transactions.
4. **What happens on deadlock?** A JV needs a deadlock clause with a buy-sell mechanism, not a promise to negotiate.
5. **Can you replace the general manager?** If the partner's nominee runs operations, the answer may be no.

:::note
A nominee shareholder — a Chinese individual holding shares for you — is not a structure. It is an undocumented trust. If you use one, you need a written entrustment agreement, a share pledge and a lawyer, and you are still exposed.
:::

## When the partnership is the right vehicle

A foreign-invested partnership (FIP) is a partnership, not a company: partners have unlimited liability for the partnership's debts unless they are limited partners. It is used by funds, by professional services firms that want flow-through treatment, and in some zones for specific activities.

For an operating business that will hire, lease and sign contracts, a limited liability company is almost always the better default.

## What the tax comparison actually shows

The common claim is that a WFOE pays 25% corporate income tax while a JV or an offshore structure pays less. In practice:

- The standard corporate income tax rate is 25%; small low-profit companies can qualify for an effective 5% through 2027.
- A JV does not get a lower rate because it is a JV.
- An offshore holding company does not reduce Chinese tax on Chinese-source profits; it may reduce withholding tax on dividends under a treaty, which is a question for [the holding company guide](/guides/hong-kong-holding-company/).

Structure follows access and control. Tax planning comes after, and usually changes less than founders expect.
