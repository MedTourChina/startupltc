"Logistics" is an umbrella over five regulated activities. Each has its own licence, its own regulator and its own foreign-investment rule, and a company licensed for one may not legally perform another.

## Road freight

Domestic road transport requires:

- A **road transport operating licence** (道路运输经营许可证) from the local transport bureau, with the vehicle category on it.
- **Vehicle operating certificates** (道路运输证) for every truck.
- Drivers with the commercial freight qualification (从业资格证) and the right licence class.
- A safety management system, GPS monitoring and driver hours records.

Foreign investment in road freight has been liberalised and the activity is not on the 2024 negative list, but the operating licence is issued locally and the bureau will inspect the depot and the safety file. Confirm the equity treatment with the transport bureau before committing to a structure.

## International freight forwarding

Forwarding — arranging transport, not operating it — is a **filing**, not a licence:

- File with MOFCOM's international freight forwarding system (国际货运代理企业备案).
- No minimum capital in most cities, but a registered address and a business scope that includes 国际货运代理.
- As an NVOCC (issuing your own bills of lading), file separately with the Ministry of Transport (无船承运业务备案).
- Customs broker services need a customs filing (报关企业备案) and, for the staff, customs declarant certificates.

This is the lightest route into logistics: a foreign-owned company can forward freight without owning a truck.

## Express delivery

Courier services are a licensed activity:

- A **express business operating licence** (快递业务经营许可证) from the provincial postal administration.
- Domestic express delivery is **limited to joint ventures** for foreign investors; international express is treated differently. The negative list still carries the domestic restriction.
- Branches and末端网点 must be filed, and the licence covers the service area.
- Cross-border e-commerce parcels run under the 9610/1210 customs regimes, with the platform or logistics provider filing the清单.

A foreign founder wanting to build a last-mile network usually partners with a licensed domestic courier rather than applying.

## Warehousing

There is no warehousing licence, but there are conditions:

- **Fire acceptance** for the building and the fit-out, especially for racking, hazardous goods and cold storage.
- **Land use**: industrial or logistics zoning; warehouses in converted residential or agricultural land are a demolition risk.
- **Hazardous chemicals** need a separate operating licence and storage approval.
- **Cold chain** needs temperature monitoring and, for food, a food-related licence if you handle or repack food.
- **Bonded warehouses** are approved by the local customs office and supervised; they allow duty deferral and are the backbone of cross-border e-commerce.

## Customs and trade compliance

- Customs broker: filing, plus declarant staff.
- AEO certification: voluntary, and valuable — lower inspection rates and faster clearance.
- Processing trade manuals, if importing materials duty-free for re-export.
- Origin management, if goods are re-exported to markets with tariffs or anti-dumping duties.

## What the customer actually buys

| The customer needs | The licence that covers it |
|---|---|
| A truck moving goods across the city | Road transport operating licence |
| A forwarder booking sea freight and clearing customs | Forwarding filing + customs broker filing |
| A parcel delivered to a consumer | Express business licence |
| Pallet storage and picking | Warehousing (fire approval, zoning) |
| Duty-deferred storage before e-commerce sale | Bonded warehouse approval |

:::warn
The most common structural error is a forwarding company that starts running its own trucks to "control quality". That is a different licensed activity, and a road transport licence is not granted to a company whose depot and safety file have not been inspected. Subcontract the haulage until the volume justifies the licence.
:::

| Road transport licence, with two trucks | ¥150,000–400,000 plus vehicles |

## Building the operating model

1. Decide whether you are a forwarder (asset-light), a carrier (licensed vehicles), a warehouse operator or a courier.
2. License that activity, and subcontract everything else.
3. Get the customs filings before the first import or export — not after.
4. Price with demurrage, detention and customs delays included.
5. Keep the safety and driver records that the transport bureau will ask for.

:::checklist
- Activity defined: forwarding, haulage, warehousing, express or brokerage
- Licence or filing confirmed for that activity, in writing
- Foreign equity rule checked for road transport and express
- Depot, vehicles and safety file ready for inspection
- Customs broker filing and declarant staff in place
- Bonded warehouse approval, if duty deferral is in the model
- Insurance: cargo, liability and vehicle
:::
