"Media" is not one permission. It is at least six, and the negative list separates them sharply. A foreign founder can build a real business in content — as a vendor to licensed publishers, not as the publisher.

## The line the law draws

| Activity | Foreign ownership | The licence |
|---|---|---|
| News gathering and news agencies | Prohibited | — |
| Book, newspaper, periodical, audio-visual and electronic publication editing | Prohibited | Publishing licence held by a Chinese entity |
| Radio and TV programme production and operation | Restricted, Chinese control | 广播电视节目制作经营许可证 |
| Film production | Restricted, Chinese control | 电影制片 / 摄制许可 |
| Film distribution | Restricted; imported films via designated state distributors | 电影发行经营许可证 |
| Online publishing (e-books, games, digital content) | Chinese-controlled entity | 网络出版服务许可证 |
| Online audio-visual platforms | Chinese-controlled; very few licences | 信息网络传播视听节目许可证 |
| Internet news services | Chinese-controlled only | 互联网新闻信息服务许可证 |
| Advertising, marketing, design, production services | Open | Business scope only |
| MCN / creator services | Open as a service company | Business scope; contracts with platforms |

The pattern: **the regulated activity must sit in a Chinese-controlled licensed entity**. Foreign capital can own the service company around it — production, editing, design, marketing, technology — but not the publishing permission itself.

## What "Chinese control" means in practice

- For broadcast and film production, the licence holder must be Chinese-controlled. A 49/51 joint venture with a Chinese partner is the standard shape.
- The licence is not transferable and not franchisable. A "licence partnership" where the foreign side effectively runs the licensed entity is a compliance risk for both sides.
- Online publishing and audio-visual licences are effectively unavailable to new foreign-invested applicants; plan around them rather than for them.

## Games

Games are the largest content export business and the most structured:

- A game needs an ISBN (版号) approved by the national press and publication regulator before commercial release.
- A foreign game is published through a licensed Chinese publisher; the publisher holds the approval and takes a share.
- approvals are slow and unpredictable; the pipeline, not the launch date, is what you manage.
- Minors' protection rules (play-time limits, real-name verification, spending caps) are enforced on the operator, and the publisher passes them down.
- An online game operation also needs ICP and, depending on the model, a cultural business licence.

## Music, video and live streaming

- Recorded music can be licensed and distributed through Chinese partners; live performance agencies need a performance licence (营业性演出许可证).
- Short-video and live-stream platforms need audio-visual and cultural licences that foreign entities cannot hold. An MCN is a service company: it signs creators, produces content and buys traffic — no platform licence.
- Live commerce adds advertising and e-commerce obligations: the host is an advertiser, the MCN may be an advertising operator, and the platform has its own duties.
- Content rules apply: the prohibited-content list is broad, and platforms enforce it before regulators do. Build a review process rather than arguing afterwards.

## The realistic business models

1. **Production services**: filming, editing, animation, post-production, design, for Chinese and foreign brands. Open, and quality is the differentiator.
2. **Marketing and content agency**: strategy, social accounts, KOL campaigns. Open; the client holds the regulated activity.
3. **Creator management (MCN)**: contracts, production, commerce. Open as a service business.
4. **Licensed publishing partnership**: a Chinese publisher holds the ISBN/licence; you supply content, brand and technology under contract.
5. **Technology for publishers**: CMS, rights management, analytics, ad tech sold to licensed entities.
6. **Game co-development or publishing support**: you build, a licensed Chinese publisher approves and operates.

## The traps

- Treating a Chinese partner's licence as if it were yours. It is not; the partner can lose it, and with it your business.
- Launching an app with user-generated content without an audio-visual or online publishing licence.
- Assuming an MCN can run a streaming platform. It cannot.
- Importing content rights without checking whether the title can be distributed at all.
- Using "news" framing in marketing content; the line between commentary and news gathering matters.

:::checklist
- The activity classified: news, publishing, broadcast, film, platform, or service
- Licence holder identified, and it is Chinese-controlled where required
- The contract makes clear who holds the licence and what happens if it is lost
- Content review process in place before launch
- Game approvals, if any, treated as a pipeline with dates
- Advertising claims checked against the absolute-claims rules
:::
