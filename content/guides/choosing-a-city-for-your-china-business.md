Founders often pick Shanghai or Beijing by default, then discover that the business is in Shenzhen, or that the rent in Shanghai would have paid two years of runway in Chengdu. The city decision is worth an afternoon of structured thinking, because it is expensive to reverse: moving a registered company means deregistering in one place and registering again in another, with a bank account and a tax record to rebuild.

## What does not differ much

- The company form and the registration portal.
- The five-year paid-in capital rule.
- The tax rates — VAT, CIT and withholding are national.
- The negative list, which applies nationwide (free trade zones differ slightly).
- The annual report and audit obligations.

So the differences are operational, not legal.

## The four variables

**1. Cost of premises and salaries.** Office rent varies by a factor of three across the ten cities on this site, and salaries by 30–50% for the same role. A team of ten in Shanghai can cost 1.5–2 times the same team in Chengdu or Xi'an, mostly in salary and social insurance.

**2. Talent pool by function.** Each city has a specialisation:

| City | Strongest for |
|---|---|
| Shanghai | Finance, professional services, consumer brands, regional HQ |
| Beijing | Policy, media, research, state-linked customers, tech |
| Shenzhen | Hardware, electronics, supply chain, cross-border e-commerce |
| Guangzhou | Trade, consumer goods, logistics, Africa and Southeast Asia trade |
| Hangzhou | E-commerce, platforms, consumer internet, fintech |
| Chengdu | Software, games, cost-efficient operations, consumer |
| Suzhou | Manufacturing, biotech, industrial parks, Shanghai overflow |
| Wuhan | Optics, automotive, education, engineering talent |
| Chongqing | Automotive, electronics, logistics, lower-cost manufacturing |
| Xi'an | Semiconductors, aerospace, engineering, universities |

If your first hires are hardware engineers, Shenzhen or Suzhou; if they are quant analysts, Shanghai; if they are game developers, Chengdu or Hangzhou. Hiring outside the cluster means paying a premium or relocating people.

**3. Registration friction.** Name approval, address requirements and the reading of business scope vary in strictness. Some districts accept a virtual address for a consulting company; others insist on a physical office for the same scope. A district with a sector focus (a biotech park, an e-commerce park) is usually faster for the companies it wants.

**4. Sector cluster and customers.** Proximity to customers still matters in China, especially for B2B sales, licensing and government-facing work. A software company selling to state-owned enterprises will find Beijing or Shanghai easier than Chengdu, regardless of cost.

## A cost comparison that means something

Use the same basket in every city:

- One registered address (serviced office, two desks).
- One general manager, one engineer, one salesperson, one accountant.
- Bookkeeping and audit.
- Social insurance at local rates.

The site's [city pages](/cities/) give the address band, typical setup time and the four local variables for each city. Plug the salaries into the [setup cost tool](/tools/setup-cost/) with the city factor to see the first-year total. The ranking rarely changes: Shanghai and Beijing at the top, Shenzhen and Hangzhou close behind, Chengdu, Wuhan, Chongqing and Xi'an 30–40% lower.

## Mistakes worth avoiding

- **Choosing by airport.** A hub is convenient for you, not for the business. You will fly monthly, not daily.
- **Choosing the cheapest city, then hiring remotely.** Remote management across provinces works for engineering, badly for sales, and not at all for licences and inspections.
- **Registering in a park for a subsidy you have not read.** Grants are contractual, often paid after milestones, and sometimes clawed back if you leave early.
- **Assuming a Shanghai address makes you a Shanghai company for tax.** Tax is administered where you register and where you operate; a shell address in a low-tax park with staff elsewhere creates risk.
- **Forgetting the founder's own permit.** Work and residence permits are issued locally, and some cities are stricter about a founder working in a city other than the one where the company is registered.

## The decision in one page

1. Write down the three roles you will hire first and the three customers you will call first.
2. Check whether those roles and customers are concentrated in one city.
3. Price the same basket in the two leading candidates.
4. Visit both, meet the local registration agent and one candidate hire.
5. Register where the answer to step 1 is strongest, unless the cost difference is decisive.

:::checklist
- First three hires identified, with the city where they are easiest to find
- First three customers identified, with their location
- Two candidate cities priced with the same basket
- Local address and business-scope requirements confirmed in writing
- Founder's work permit path checked for the chosen city
- Any subsidy offer read in full, including clawback terms
:::
