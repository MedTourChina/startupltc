China's factories will make almost anything, and that is the easy part. The hard parts are knowing which factory is real, who owns the mould, and what happens when the container is wrong.

## Build or buy

Foreign investment in manufacturing is not restricted — the negative list's manufacturing entries were reduced to zero. But building a factory is a capital project, not a market entry. Most first-time founders should buy:

- **OEM**: you specify the product, the factory makes it to your design.
- **ODM**: the factory has a design and you brand it. Cheaper, less defensible, and the same product may appear under other brands.

A trading company in between is not automatically bad — some of the best suppliers are trading companies with reliable factory networks — but you should know which one you are dealing with.

## Verifying a supplier

1. **Business licence and scope.** The company must be registered and its business scope must include manufacturing of the product. A company whose scope is "sales" is a trader.
2. **Factory audit.** Walk the line, count the machines, check the QC station, look at the warehouse. A video call from a showroom proves nothing.
3. **Certifications.** ISO 9001 is a baseline, not a guarantee. Ask which certification body and check the certificate number.
4. **References.** Two customers you can call, ideally outside China.
5. **Sample and test.** Test the sample against your own specification, not the factory's.
6. **Third-party inspection.** SGS, TÜV, Bureau Veritas or a local QC firm, paid by you, reporting to you.

:::warn
The most expensive supplier is the one that is cheap and plausible. A factory that cannot show you its production licence, its environmental permits and its export records is a risk you cannot price.
:::

## Tooling and IP

- **Pay for the tooling yourself** and put ownership in the contract: the mould, the fixtures and the drawings belong to you, are marked as yours, and must be returned or destroyed on termination.
- **Register the trademark in China before the first sample**, and register the design patent or utility model where relevant. First-to-file means the factory — or its neighbour — can register your brand while you negotiate.
- **Use a manufacturing agreement** with confidentiality, non-compete for your design, IP assignment, and an indemnity if the factory sells your product to others.
- **Record the trademark with customs** so counterfeit exports can be stopped at the border.
- **Expect the design to leak.** Assume a competing listing will appear within a season; the defence is brand, distribution and speed, not secrecy alone.

## The purchase contract

What to specify:

- Product specification, golden sample, and the standard the goods are tested against.
- Inspection method and AQL level, and who pays for the inspection.
- Packaging, labelling and carton marks.
- Delivery date, with a penalty for late delivery and a cap on liability.
- Payment terms and currency.
- Warranty period and the remedy for defects.
- Tooling ownership and return.
- IP ownership, confidentiality and indemnity.
- Governing law and forum — Hong Kong arbitration or CIETAC are the practical choices.

## Payment terms

- **30% deposit, 70% before shipment** is standard for a first order. It is also the point where buyers lose money.
- **Letter of credit** for large orders, though Chinese banks handle them routinely.
- **Escrow or platform protection** (for example Alibaba Trade Assurance) for smaller orders.
- **Inspection before the balance**, written into the contract, is the single most effective protection.

## Export, VAT and origin

If you buy from a Chinese factory and export:

- The factory issues a VAT special invoice; the export declaration is made by an export agent or your own foreign trade company.
- Export VAT refund rates depend on the HS code — commonly 13% or 9%, sometimes 0%. A wrong HS code is a refund loss or a compliance problem.
- The export declaration establishes origin. "Substantial transformation" decides whether goods made in China from imported parts count as Chinese origin — relevant for tariffs and anti-dumping duties in the destination market.
- Processing trade (importing materials duty-free for re-export) is a separate customs regime with bonded supervision; use it only with an agent who does it routinely.

## Inspection, in practice

| Stage | What to check |
|---|---|
| Pre-production | Materials, components, the golden sample signed and kept |
| During production | First-article inspection, line audit at 20–30% completion |
| Pre-shipment | Random sample to AQL, packaging and labels, carton drop test |
| On arrival | Count, damage, and a sample against the golden sample |

## Red flags

- A price 30% below every other quote.
- No factory tour, or a tour of a "sample room" only.
- Refusal to sign an IP clause.
- Request to pay to a personal account.
- Units that appear in another brand's listing with the same tooling marks.

:::checklist
- Business licence and manufacturing scope verified
- Factory audited in person or by a paid third party
- Tooling paid by you, ownership and return in the contract
- Trademark filed in China before launch, customs recordal planned
- Inspection booked before the balance payment
- HS code and export VAT refund rate confirmed
- Contract covers IP, late delivery, defects and forum
:::
