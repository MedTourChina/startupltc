Registration is a project. Bookkeeping is a habit. The companies that get into trouble are rarely the ones that missed a registration step; they are the ones that treated the books as something to sort out at year end.

## What the law actually requires

- **Books in Chinese**, recorded in RMB, following the accounting standards applicable to the company.
- **Vouchers and records retained for at least ten years** — accounting books, fapiao, contracts, bank statements, payroll records.
- **Monthly or quarterly filing** of VAT and surcharges, monthly withholding of individual income tax, quarterly prepayment of corporate income tax.
- **An annual corporate income tax reconciliation** by 31 May of the following year.
- **The annual report** to the market regulator by 30 June.

For a foreign-invested company, add the annual audit by a Chinese CPA firm. It is not optional in practice: the bank, the tax bureau and any dividend remittance will ask for audited financial statements, and a company without them cannot move profit out.

## The year in one table

| When | What | Who files |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly, by the 15th | VAT and surcharges, individual income tax withholding, social insurance | Bookkeeper or tax agent |
| Quarterly, by the 15th | Corporate income tax prepayment, VAT for small-scale taxpayers | Bookkeeper |
| By 31 March | Individual income tax annual reconciliation opens | Employer and employee |
| By 30 April | Audit fieldwork and signed audit report (practical deadline) | CPA firm |
| By 31 May | Corporate income tax annual reconciliation | Bookkeeper or tax agent |
| By 30 June | Individual income tax reconciliation closes; annual report to the market regulator | Employer / company |
| Ongoing | Fapiao issued and received, contracts, payroll, bank slips | Everyone |

Dates shift when they fall on a public holiday, and local practice on the audit deadline varies. Treat the table as the planning frame, not the legal text.

## The audit

A Chinese CPA firm issues the audit report. Expect it to ask for:

- Full-year bank statements, reconciled to the books.
- Sales and purchase contracts, matched to fapiao.
- Payroll, social insurance and housing fund records.
- Related-party transactions, with pricing support.
- Capital contribution evidence — bank slips into the capital account and, for in-kind contributions, the valuation.
- Inventory counts for trading and manufacturing companies.
- Shareholder loans with written agreements.

The report goes to the company, the tax bureau if requested, and the bank when profit is remitted. A qualified opinion — for example, where capital is subscribed but not paid — can block a dividend.

:::warn
Capital contribution is the most common audit finding for foreign-founded companies. The five-year clock runs from incorporation, and an unpaid subscription shows up in the audit, in the annual report and in the bank's review. If the money is not coming, reduce the registered capital properly rather than let it sit unpaid.
:::

## What auditors and tax inspectors find

1. **Expenses without fapiao** — a payment with a receipt but no invoice is usually not deductible.
2. **Shareholder loans with no agreement** — cash moving between the owner and the company is either capital, a loan or a dividend, and each has different tax treatment.
3. **Related-party pricing** — services billed from the offshore parent at a markup that does not match the function performed.
4. **Revenue timing** — invoices issued late or not at all, so reported revenue lags the contracts.
5. **Payroll outside the books** — salaries paid in cash or through a third party, with no individual income tax withheld.
6. **Personal expenses in the company** — cars, rent, travel for the owner, booked as business costs.
7. **Missing inventory** — stock bought and sold with no count and no cost of goods records.

## What it costs

| Service | Typical monthly or annual cost |
|---|---|
| Bookkeeping, small company, few transactions | ¥500–1,500 / month |
| Bookkeeping, payroll and filings included | ¥1,500–3,000 / month |
| Annual audit, small company | ¥5,000–15,000 |
| Annual audit, revenue above ¥10m or inventory | ¥15,000–40,000 |
| Tax agent for the annual reconciliation | ¥2,000–6,000 |
| Registered tax representative (where used) | ¥3,000–8,000 / year |

Cheap bookkeeping is a real market, and it is usually fine until the first audit. The failure mode is not the monthly fee; it is a year of unreconciled bank statements discovered in April.

## Choosing the bookkeeper

- Ask whether they have handled foreign-invested companies and English-language reporting.
- Confirm who signs the tax filings and what happens when they leave.
- Keep your own copies of the bank statements and the fapiao list every month.
- Never let the bookkeeper hold the only copy of the accounting software data.

:::checklist
- Bookkeeping contract in place before the first invoice
- Monthly filing calendar with named responsible person
- Bank statements reconciled monthly, not annually
- Capital contribution evidence filed, with the payment schedule tracked
- Audit firm engaged before 31 March, not in May
- Records archived for ten years, in Chinese, backed up off-site
:::
