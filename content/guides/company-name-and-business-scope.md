Two of the smallest decisions at registration — the name and the business scope — cause the most rework later. A name that conflicts is rejected; a scope that is too narrow blocks an invoice; a scope that is too broad invites a compliance question the company cannot answer.

Neither decision is hard, but both are easier to understand before the application than after.

## The name has four parts

A standard limited liability company name looks like this:

**Shanghai** (administrative division) + **Beichen** (trade name, 字号) + **Technology** (industry) + **Co., Ltd.** (organisational form).

| Part | Rules | Practical effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Administrative division | City or district where the company is registered | Ties the name to the registry; a later move may require renaming |
| Trade name (字号) | Two or more characters, not identical or similar to an existing name in the same industry and jurisdiction | The part you actually choose; checked against the registry database |
| Industry | Describes the main business, drawn from a standard list | Should match the first item in the business scope |
| Organisational form | 有限公司, 股份有限公司, etc. | Determines liability and governance |

Foreign investors can use a Chinese translation of their foreign name as the trade name, but it must still pass the conflict check. The business licence is in Chinese; an English name is not on the licence.

:::warn
An English brand name does not automatically become your Chinese company name, and registering the company does not register the trademark. If the trade name matters commercially, file the trademark separately — see [the IP guide](/guides/trademark-and-ip-in-china/).
:::

## How the name is checked

China replaced pre-approval with **self-declaration (自主申报)** in most jurisdictions: the applicant searches the registry system, declares that the name does not conflict, and the registry reviews it as part of registration.

The check is against:

- Existing company names in the same administrative division and industry.
- Well-known marks and names protected under the Trademark Law and the Anti-Unfair Competition Law.
- Words that mislead or imply state approval — 中国, 中华, 国家, 国际 — which need special approval.
- Names that are identical or similar enough to confuse.

A name that passes the system can still be rejected on review, and a name that conflicts can still be challenged later. Self-declaration shifts the risk to the applicant.

## Choosing a name that survives

- **Avoid over-description.** "Shanghai Best Fast Reliable Consulting Co., Ltd." is forgettable and likely to conflict.
- **Use two or three characters.** Longer names are harder to remember and more likely to collide.
- **Check the pinyin and the characters for unfortunate readings.** A name that sounds wrong to a native speaker is a permanent liability.
- **Check the Chinese characters as a trademark too**, not just as a company name.
- **Reserve the name early.** A name is not held until the registration is filed.

## The business scope

The scope (经营范围) is written from the regulator's **standard catalogue of business scope expressions**, not free text. It separates:

- **General items (一般项目)** — you can start operating once registered.
- **Licensed items (许可项目)** — you need an approval before you can operate; the licence is either 前置 (before registration) or 后置 (after registration, before operating).

Most licences are post-registration. Pre-registration approvals are rare and mostly financial.

| Example item | Type | Approval |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Technology consulting; software development | General | None |
| Goods import and export | General | Declarant registration with customs |
| Food sales | Licensed (post) | Food business licence |
| Catering services | Licensed (post) | Food business licence, premises inspection |
| Education and training | Licensed (post) | Education bureau approval, sector rules |
| Medical services | Licensed (pre/post) | Health commission approval |

## Why the wording matters

The scope is read by four parties, and each reads it differently:

1. **The registry** checks that licensed items have approvals attached.
2. **The bank** checks that the business story matches the scope before opening an account.
3. **The tax bureau** checks that the scope supports the invoices you issue — an invoice for "software development" from a company whose scope is "consulting" invites a query.
4. **Customers and procurement departments** check that your scope covers what they are buying, especially in state-owned and large corporate supply chains.

A scope that says only "consulting" will struggle to invoice software, and a scope that says "all legal business" (a real formulation in some systems) satisfies nobody.

## Too narrow versus too broad

| Approach | Risk |
| --- | --- |
| Narrow, precise | Cannot invoice new lines of business without a scope change |
| Broad, vague | Bank and tax queries; licensed activities listed without licences |
| Everything | Looks unfocused; may trigger review; still does not cover licensed items |

The practical answer is: the main business first, in the standard wording; two or three adjacent activities you might invoice in year one; and licensed items only if you actually hold the licence or are about to apply.

## Changing the scope later

A scope change is a registration change (变更登记): shareholder resolution, amended articles, application, and a new licence. It typically takes one to two weeks and costs little if you do it yourself; an agent may charge ¥1,000–3,000.

It is not hard, but it is disruptive: banks may re-review the account, and customers notice a licence that no longer matches the contract.

:::note
Add the scope line before you sign the contract, not after. A customer's procurement team will ask for a licence that matches the invoice, and "we are changing it" is a poor answer.
:::

## The registration sequence

1. Choose the trade name and check it against the registry and trademark databases.
2. Choose the registered address — the name's administrative division usually follows it.
3. Draft the scope from the standard catalogue.
4. Decide registered capital and the five-year schedule.
5. File the registration: name, address, scope, capital, shareholders, legal representative, supervisors, articles.
6. Collect the licence, then carve the seals and open the bank account.

The name and scope are decided at step 1 and 3, but they constrain everything after.

:::checklist
- Trade name checked against existing companies and trademarks in the same industry
- Chinese name checked by native speakers for meaning and pronunciation
- Scope built from the standard catalogue, main business first
- Licensed items listed only where a licence exists or is being applied for
- Scope covers the invoices the company will actually issue in year one
- English name and trademark filed separately if they matter commercially
:::

:::rule
The name is a legal identifier and the scope is a permission list. Choose both for what the company will actually do in its first two years, and change them before they block a contract — not after.
:::
