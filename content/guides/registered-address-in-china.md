Every Chinese company has a registered address printed on its business licence. Founders treat it as a line on a form. It is closer to a physical precondition: the address decides whether the company can open a bank account, hold a licence and survive an inspection.

## Three audiences, three tests

The same address is checked by three different institutions, for three different reasons.

**The registry** checks that the address exists, that the property is zoned for the registered activity, and that the landlord's documentation supports the registration. Requirements vary by city; most ask for a property certificate, a lease and sometimes a landlord consent form.

**The bank** checks that the company actually operates there. Banks are required to apply know-your-customer and anti-money-laundering checks, and a registered address with no sign of the business is a red flag. Some branches visit; most ask for utility bills, a lease and photographs.

**The sector authority** checks that the premises meet licence conditions. A restaurant needs extraction, drainage and waste contracts; a clinic needs the right layout and infection control; a school needs classrooms and safety; a factory needs an environmental assessment and zoning.

An address that satisfies one test and fails another is the most common way a founder loses a year.

:::note
A "virtual address" in China usually means a shared registration address provided by an agency, not a mailbox. It can be lawful where the district permits it, but it is weak evidence for a bank, and it cannot support a licence that requires premises.
:::

## What a compliant address looks like

For a service company, a compliant address usually means:

- A lease in the company's name (or a sublease the registry accepts), stamped and dated.
- A property certificate or ownership document from the landlord.
- Zoning that permits office or commercial use.
- A real desk, a sign with the company name, and a person who answers the phone.
- Utility bills or a management fee invoice in the company's name.

For a licensed business, add the physical requirements of the licence. For a factory, add the environmental impact assessment, fire approval, safety assessment and the landlord's permission for industrial use.

## The cost range

Address costs vary enormously by city and by district. As a rough guide for a small service company, as of late 2026:

| Arrangement | Typical annual cost | Bank acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| Agency virtual address | ¥3,000–¥8,000 | Often refused |
| Shared serviced office | ¥8,000–¥25,000 | Usually accepted |
| Private office (small) | ¥30,000–¥120,000 | Accepted |
| Licensed premises (F&B, clinic) | Fit-out plus rent | Required |

The cheapest option is not the cheapest outcome. A ¥5,000 address that costs you an account, a licence and two months is a bad trade against a ¥15,000 serviced office that works.

## Address and business scope

The registry may refuse an activity at an address that is not zoned for it. Restaurants in residential buildings, clinics in office towers, factories in commercial parks and schools in industrial zones are common refusals. The zoning and the scope must agree before you sign the lease.

If you plan to change activity later, check whether the new scope is permitted at the same address. A move is a registration change, a bank update and often a licence re-inspection.

## Moving, and the cost of a bad address

Changing the registered address is a registration change: a shareholder resolution, an amended lease, a filing, a new licence, bank updates and, for licensed businesses, a re-inspection. It takes weeks and can interrupt invoicing.

More seriously, an address that cannot be verified can put the company on the **abnormal operations list**, which freezes bank changes and can block tenders. Getting off the list requires proof of a real address and a filing — usually with the same landlord who caused the problem.

:::checklist
- Lease in the company's name, stamped, with the property certificate.
- Zoning permits the registered activity.
- The bank has confirmed the address will pass onboarding, ideally in writing.
- Any sector licence's premises requirements are met before signing.
- Utility bills or management fee invoices will be available in the company's name.
:::

## Questions to ask before signing

- Is the property zoned for my activity, and can you show the certificate?
- Will you provide a lease and a landlord consent that the registry accepts?
- Has a foreign-owned company registered at this address before, and did its bank account open?
- If I need a sector licence, has this address passed that inspection before?
- What happens if the registry rejects the address — is the deposit refundable?

A landlord who cannot answer these questions is not necessarily dishonest. They may simply have never had a foreign tenant. Either way, the risk is yours.

:::rule
Sign the lease only after the registry, the bank and — if relevant — the licence authority have each indicated the address will pass. The lease is the last document, not the first.
:::

The next layer is structure: which vehicle, and who controls it. The address will follow you there, because it is printed on the licence.
