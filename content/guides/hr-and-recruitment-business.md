Recruitment looks like a service business with no licence. In China, the moment you charge a candidate or an employer for placement, you are in a licensed activity.

## The three activities

| Activity | Permission | Regulator |
|---|---|---|
| Job placement / headhunting (职业中介) | Human resources service licence (人力资源服务许可证) | County-level human resources and social security bureau |
| Other HR services — assessment, training, network recruitment | Filing (备案) | Same bureau |
| Labour dispatch (劳务派遣) | Labour dispatch operating licence (劳务派遣经营许可证) | Municipal bureau |

The licence is issued against a place of business, a management system and a responsible person. Conditions vary by city: some require a minimum floor area, a certain number of certified staff, or a deposit.

## Foreign ownership

The 2024 negative list does not list human resources services, so a wholly foreign-owned agency is possible in principle. In practice:

- The licence is issued locally, and some cities apply additional scrutiny to foreign-invested applicants.
- Talent introduction agencies working with foreigners may face extra requirements on data and on the foreign staff's own permits.
- Get the local bureau's requirements in writing before you lease an office.

## Labour dispatch, and why it is the hard licence

Dispatch — employing people and sending them to work under a client's direction — is the most regulated part of the market:

- Registered capital of at least **¥2 million**, paid in and verified.
- A licence from the municipal bureau, after an on-site check of the premises and the management system.
- Dispatch is allowed only for **temporary, auxiliary or substitute** positions.
- The number of dispatched workers may not exceed **10%** of the client's total workforce.
- The dispatch agreement, the employment contracts and the social insurance must all be consistent.

The 10% cap is why large clients structure work as outsourcing rather than dispatch.

## Outsourcing versus dispatch

- **Dispatch**: you employ the person, the client directs the work, and the person is counted in the client's 10%.
- **Outsourcing (人力资源服务外包)**: you take on a deliverable and manage your own people to deliver it. No dispatch licence, no 10% cap — but the contract and the actual management must match, or an inspection will reclassify it as dispatch.

The distinction is about who directs the work, not what the contract is called. Invoices saying "outsourcing" with timesheets and client instructions are evidence of dispatch.

## Payroll and social insurance agencies

Many small agencies earn fees by running payroll and social insurance for clients. Two problems:

- Paying social insurance for someone with no employment relationship with you is **social insurance fraud (挂靠)**, and enforcement has tightened.
- Invoicing "HR services" for what is really a salary pass-through creates VAT and individual income tax exposure for the client.

The compliant model is a genuine employment relationship or a compliant dispatch/outsourcing arrangement.

## Headhunting economics

- Contingency search fees run **20–25% of annual gross salary**; retained search is higher.
- In-house recruiting is cheaper at volume but needs a licence only if you are placing people with other employers.
- Candidate data is personal information: PIPL applies, including to CVs of foreign candidates stored overseas.
- Poaching clauses and non-competes are enforceable only within limits, and the employer must pay compensation during the non-compete period.

## A compliant setup

1. Choose the activity: placement, HR services, dispatch or outsourcing.
2. Get the licence or filing before invoicing, with the business scope to match.
3. For dispatch, capitalise at ¥2 million and prepare for the on-site inspection.
4. Draft contracts that reflect who actually directs the work.
5. Handle candidate and employee data under PIPL, with a retention rule.
6. Invoice the service actually delivered, at arm's length.

:::checklist
- Activity mapped to the correct licence or filing
- Licence issued against the real office, before the first invoice
- Dispatch: ¥2 million capital, 10% cap understood, contracts consistent
- Outsourcing contracts describe deliverables, not headcount
- No social insurance paid for people with no employment relationship
- Candidate data handling documented under PIPL
:::
