There is a gap in most guides between "register a company" and "be allowed to run it". A foreign founder can own a Chinese company from abroad indefinitely — the company does not need a foreigner on the payroll. What requires a permit is you doing the work in China.

## The visas, plainly

| Visa | What it is for | Can you work on it? |
|---|---|---|
| Visa-free entry (30 days, many nationalities) | Tourism, family, short business visits | No |
| 240-hour visa-free transit | Connecting through China, short visit in a region | No |
| L | Tourism | No |
| M | Business and trade: meetings, inspections, signing | No employment, but commercial visits are fine |
| F | Exchanges, study tours, non-commercial visits | No |
| Z | Work | Yes, after conversion to a residence permit |
| R | High-level talent | Yes |
| APEC Business Travel Card | Multiple short business entries | No employment |

The distinction that catches people is between *commercial activity* — meetings, negotiation, due diligence, signing — and *employment*, which means being paid for work performed in China or running the company day to day from a Chinese office.

## What the 30-day visa-free entry is good for

For passport holders covered by the unilateral arrangements, entry is straightforward and needs no invitation letter. It is genuinely useful for a first trip: see the city, meet the registry agent, inspect offices, open a bank introduction.

It is not a way to run the business. Immigration police treat repeated short entries followed by local work as unauthorised employment, and the penalties fall on both the individual and the company that benefited.

:::warn
A common pattern that ends badly: founder enters visa-free, registers the company, starts invoicing, and stays past 30 days. Overstay plus unauthorised work is a fine and possible deportation, with an entry ban that can run for years. Convert to a proper status before revenue starts.
:::

## The Z visa route

The work permit belongs to the employer, so the sequence is:

1. **The company is registered** and has its licence, seals and tax registration.
2. **The employer applies for the work permit notification** (外国人工作许可通知) through the local science and technology bureau, usually one to three weeks.
3. **You apply for the Z visa** at a Chinese embassy or consulate with the notification, passport and supporting documents.
4. **You enter** and, within 30 days, complete the medical check and convert to the work permit card and residence permit.

Documents the work permit application will want:

- Passport with enough validity.
- Bachelor's degree or above, and two years of relevant work experience for a Category B permit.
- Clean criminal record check, apostilled or legalised, usually dated within six months.
- Medical examination, often done in China after arrival for the residence permit stage.
- Employment contract and the company's registration documents.

Category A permits — high earners, scientists, entrepreneurs with a track record — skip some requirements, including the degree and experience tests in defined cases. Most first-time founders fall into B.

## Founders versus employees

There is a specific route for entrepreneurs that some cities operate: an innovation and entrepreneurship category, or a points-based assessment that credits investment, patents, revenue and local recognition. It is city-specific and changes; ask the local foreign expert bureau rather than relying on a blog.

If the founder is also the legal representative, that does not by itself give a right to work. The permit is still required, and the legal representative's presence is expected for bank and registry steps regardless.

## Family

- **S1/S2** — spouse and children of a work-permit holder; S1 for stays over 180 days, converted to a residence permit.
- **Q1/Q2** — family of Chinese citizens or permanent residents.

Bring the marriage and birth certificates, apostilled and translated, and start early: family permits are usually processed after the principal's residence permit exists.

## Visa runs do not work

Leaving to Hong Kong or Seoul and re-entering resets a visa-free stay, not your status. Immigration systems record continuous patterns, and a founder who has been in China for months on tourist entries while the company operates is visible in the data.

The same applies to "I'll just use my Hong Kong company" — a Hong Kong company can own the mainland company, but it does not give you a right to live or work in the mainland.

## A realistic sequence for a first-time founder

| Month | Status | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Visa-free or M | Visit, choose city, meet agents and banks |
| 1 | Back home | Prepare apostilled documents, register remotely or via agent |
| 2 | M visa | Sign lease, open bank file, submit work permit notification |
| 3 | Z visa | Enter, medical, work permit card, residence permit |
| 3+ | Residence permit | Run the company lawfully, hire, invoice |

:::checklist
- Entry status matched to what you will actually do on the trip
- Criminal record check requested early — it is the slowest document
- Work permit notification filed by the company, not by you personally
- Residence permit application started within 30 days of entry
- Family documents apostilled before the principal's permit is issued
:::
