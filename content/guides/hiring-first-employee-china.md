Hiring in China is not difficult, but it is formal. The Labour Contract Law assumes a written contract, mandatory social insurance, and a termination process with evidence. Employers who treat it casually accumulate liabilities that surface at the worst moment — usually when the employee leaves or the company wants to.

## The written contract

A written labour contract must be signed **within one month** of the employee starting work. If it is not:

- After one month and before one year, the employer owes **double wages** for the period without a contract.
- After one year, the employment is treated as open-ended, and the double-wage liability applies.

The contract should state the role, location, salary, working hours, probation, and termination terms. A Chinese-language version is the one that governs; an English translation can accompany it.

## Probation

Probation is limited by contract length:

| Contract term | Maximum probation |
|---|---|
| Under 3 months | None |
| 3 months to under 1 year | 1 month |
| 1 year to under 3 years | 2 months |
| 3 years or open-ended | 6 months |

Probation can be used only once, and the probation salary cannot be below 80% of the agreed salary or the local minimum wage. Terminating during probation still requires a reason and evidence.

## Social insurance and housing fund

Employers must enrol employees in the five social insurances — pension, medical, unemployment, work injury and maternity — and the housing provident fund. Contributions are a percentage of the contribution base, shared between employer and employee, and the rates vary by city.

The employer share is commonly around **25–30%** of the contribution base, plus **5–12%** for the housing fund. The base is capped and adjusted annually, usually in July.

:::warn
"Employee agrees to waive social insurance" clauses are void. An employee can later claim the contributions, and the employer faces back payments, late fees and possible fines. The same applies to paying salary into a personal account to avoid contributions.
:::

## Salary, tax and payroll

The employer withholds individual income tax monthly and files the payroll. IIT is progressive, with the standard deduction of ¥5,000 per month plus specific deductions for housing loan interest or rent, children's education, elderly care and continuing education.

Foreign employees may qualify for additional allowances, but the old tax-exempt allowance regime was phased out; most employers now simply gross up the salary.

## Working hours and overtime

The standard system is **8 hours a day, 40 hours a week**, with at least one rest day per week. Overtime is capped at **36 hours a month** in principle, and must be paid at:

- **150%** of hourly wage on working days.
- **200%** on rest days, unless compensatory time is given.
- **300%** on statutory holidays.

Some roles use the comprehensive working-hour system, which requires approval. "Voluntary overtime" without pay is a common practice and a common claim.

## Annual leave

Employees with one to ten years of cumulative service get **5 days** of paid annual leave; ten to twenty years, **10 days**; twenty years or more, **15 days**. Unused leave must be compensated at 300% of the daily wage (200% on top of normal pay in practice).

## Termination

Termination is where most disputes arise. The lawful routes:

- **Mutual agreement**, in writing, with severance.
- **Employee resignation** with 30 days' notice (3 days during probation).
- **Employer termination for cause** — serious misconduct, proven incompetence after training or reassignment, material breach. Requires evidence and, for incompetence, a documented process.
- **Redundancy** — restructuring, major technical change or economic difficulty, with consultation and severance.
- **Expiry** of a fixed-term contract, with severance unless the employer offers a renewal on equal or better terms and the employee refuses.

Severance is generally **one month's salary per year of service**, with a half-month for periods of six months or more, capped at three times the local average monthly wage for high earners.

:::kill
Do not terminate an employee by stopping their access, cutting pay or pressuring a resignation. Constructive dismissal claims are common, and the employer usually loses. Use the procedure or negotiate an exit.
:::

## Budgeting the first hire

For a gross salary of ¥20,000 a month, the employer's total cost is roughly ¥26,000–¥28,000 including social insurance and housing fund, before any bonus. Add recruitment, equipment and onboarding. The rule of thumb is **1.3–1.4× gross salary**.

:::checklist
- Written contract signed within one month, Chinese version
- Probation within the legal limit for the contract term
- Social insurance and housing fund enrolled from the first month
- Payroll withholding and IIT filing set up
- Working hours, overtime and leave policies written down
- A termination procedure with evidence requirements
:::
