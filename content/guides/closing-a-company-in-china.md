Founders plan how to start a Chinese company far more carefully than how to end one. The asymmetry is expensive, because an unused company does not become harmless — it keeps accruing filing obligations, and the person on the licence keeps carrying the consequences.

## Three ways out

1. **Deregistration after liquidation** — the standard route for a company with assets, liabilities, employees or history.
2. **Simplified deregistration** — a faster route for small companies with no outstanding debts, available where the company qualifies and all shareholders consent.
3. **Sale of the entity** — transferring the equity to a buyer who wants the licence, the history or the premises. Rare, but it happens.

A fourth option, abandoning the company, is not an exit. It is a deferred problem.

## The standard liquidation route

**Step 1 — Resolution and liquidation group.** Shareholders resolve to dissolve and appoint a liquidation group. The group takes control of the company, the seal and the books.

**Step 2 — Notice and announcement.** Creditors are notified and a public announcement is published, typically for **45 days**, in the national enterprise credit system or a newspaper recognised by the registry.

**Step 3 — Liquidation.** Assets are inventoried, claims collected, debts paid, employees settled, taxes calculated. A liquidation report is prepared.

**Step 4 — Tax deregistration.** The tax authority checks filings, invoices, and outstanding liabilities, and issues a tax clearance. This is often the slowest step, especially where invoices were issued irregularly.

**Step 5 — Customs and other registrations.** If the company had customs registration or sector licences, those are cancelled too.

**Step 6 — Business licence cancellation.** The market regulator cancels the registration and the company ceases to exist as a legal person.

**Step 7 — Bank accounts and seals.** Accounts are closed and the seals destroyed or surrendered, with records kept.

## Simplified deregistration

The simplified route skips the liquidation group and the 45-day newspaper announcement, replacing it with a shorter public notice (commonly **20 days**) and a commitment that there are no outstanding debts.

It is available to companies that meet the conditions — typically small companies with no employees, no debts, no tax arrears and no异常 status. The commitment is binding: if a creditor appears later, the shareholders are liable.

:::warn
Simplified deregistration is not a loophole for a company with unpaid taxes or undisclosed debts. The shareholders sign a commitment, and the authority can revoke the cancellation and pursue them personally.
:::

## What it costs

- **Government fees:** minimal.
- **Agent or accountant:** ¥8,000–¥30,000 for a simple company, more if there are tax issues, employees or a licence.
- **Outstanding liabilities:** taxes, social insurance, severance, lease termination.
- **Time:** four to nine months for the standard route; two to four for simplified.

The largest variable is the tax clearance. A company with clean filings and no unusual invoices clears quickly; one with missing filings or invoice mismatches can take a year.

## Selling the company instead

A buyer may want the entity rather than a new one — for a licence, a registered address, a customs code or a track record. An equity transfer needs due diligence, a share purchase agreement, tax clearance, and registration of the change. The buyer inherits the history, including any hidden liabilities, so warranties and indemnities matter.

## What happens if you walk away

Abandoning a company — stopping filings without deregistering — leads to:

- The **abnormal operations list**, then the **serious violation list**.
- The legal representative being restricted from acting as legal representative or director of other companies, and in some cases restricted from leaving China.
- Shareholders being pursued for unpaid capital, taxes and liquidation obligations.
- The company's name and credit record following the individuals involved.

The restrictions attach to people, not just to the company. A founder who leaves China and never returns may not feel them immediately; a founder who wants to start another company, obtain a visa or open a bank account will.

:::kill
Never "close" a company by stopping to file and hoping. The company remains registered, the obligations continue, and the exit becomes more expensive every year.
:::

## Planning the exit

The cheapest closure is designed at the start:

- Keep filings current, so the tax clearance is simple.
- Avoid invoices that do not match contracts.
- Do not lend the legal representative role to someone outside the business.
- Keep the seal, the licence and the bank U-key under control.
- Decide early whether the entity is worth selling.

:::checklist
- All tax filings current and reconciled
- Employees settled with lawful severance
- Lease terminated and address released
- Bank accounts identified, with the U-keys
- Seals located and accounted for
- Licences and customs registration on the cancellation list
- A named person responsible for the process
:::

## The last word

A company is easy to start and slow to end, and the difference between a clean exit and a decade of problems is mostly paperwork done on time. If the business is not working, closing it deliberately is cheaper than letting it decay — and it protects the option to come back.
