"Fintech" describes a technology, not a permission. In China every financial activity is licensed, the licences are few, and the regulator looks at who bears the risk rather than what the app does.

## Payments

- Third-party payment business needs a **payment business licence** (支付业务许可证) from the People's Bank of China.
- New licences have not been issued since the 2016–2017 clean-up. The market consolidated, and entry is by acquiring a licensed company — with PBOC approval of the change of control.
- Foreign-invested payment institutions are permitted under the 2018 opening, and at least one global platform entered that way. Approval is slow and conditions are strict: local infrastructure, data localisation, and a real business presence.
- **Cross-border payment** for e-commerce is a separate authorisation, usually through a licensed payment institution rather than your own licence.
- **Aggregation and acquiring outsourcing** (聚合支付) requires filing as an outsourced service provider; it is a service to merchants, not a payment licence.

## Lending

| Model | Licence | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Small loan company (小额贷款) | Provincial financial regulator | Local lending area, leverage capped, capital requirements |
| Online small loan | National rules, provincial registration | Cross-provincial lending restricted; high capital bar |
| Consumer finance company | National financial regulator, with a bank sponsor | The realistic route for a large consumer lender |
| P2P lending | None — banned | The sector was wound down |

The pattern is that lending licences are granted to well-capitalised entities with a financial institution behind them, not to startups. A foreign founder can supply technology or servicing; owning the balance sheet is a different game.

## Investment and advice

- **Securities investment advisory** needs a CSRC licence; recommending specific securities without one is illegal.
- **Public fund management** is open to 100% foreign ownership, but the approval is for an established asset manager with a track record, not a new app.
- **Private fund management** registers with the Asset Management Association of China; registration is achievable for a serious manager.
- **Fund investment advisory** (基金投顾) is a pilot licence held by a small number of institutions.
- **Robo-advisory** without a licence is still investment advice; the software wrapper does not change the permission.

## Insurance

- Insurance companies: foreign ownership is permitted, subject to a national licence and substantial capital.
- Insurance intermediaries — brokers and agencies — are open to foreign investment, with a licence from the financial regulator.
- Insurance technology that sells policies must sit on a licensed intermediary; lead generation and comparison models need care.

## Credit and data

- Personal credit reporting needs a licence; the market is centred on a small number of approved bureaus.
- Credit scoring for lending decisions is regulated, and using alternative data to make credit decisions can bring you inside the credit reporting rules.
- Data for financial marketing is subject to PIPL and to financial consumer protection rules.

## Crypto and tokens

- Trading, exchange services, mining and token issuance are prohibited. The 2021 notice treats virtual currency activities as illegal financial activities.
- RMB stablecoins are prohibited.
- NFTs sit in a grey zone: platforms exist, but resale and financialisation attract enforcement.
- Holding or researching crypto as a foreign company is different from operating a business in China around it. The line is the activity, not the asset.

## What is actually open

- **Software for licensed institutions**: core banking modules, risk engines, compliance tooling, reconciliation, KYC utilities. You sell to a bank, not to the public.
- **Regulatory technology**: AML, sanctions screening, reporting. The buyer is licensed; you are a vendor.
- **Cross-border treasury and FX operations tooling** for corporates.
- **Data and analytics** within PIPL and credit-reporting limits.
- **Wealth technology** distributed through a licensed fund distributor or broker.

The commercial logic is unglamorous and real: licensed institutions have budgets and compliance departments, and they buy from foreign vendors.

## The structural question

If the product touches money movement, lending, advice or insurance sales, you need one of:

1. Your own licence (rare and slow).
2. A licensed partner, with the customer relationship and the regulated activity sitting inside the partner.
3. A pure vendor contract, where the licensed institution remains the operator.

Anything else — "we are only a platform", "the partner holds the money", "it is technology, not finance" — is the argument regulators have heard many times.

:::checklist
- The activity identified: payments, lending, advice, insurance, credit data, or software
- Whether a licence is needed, confirmed by counsel
- If partnering: the licensed entity's role and liability documented
- If vendor: the contract makes the institution the operator
- Crypto exposure removed from the business model
- Data flows checked against PIPL and credit-reporting rules
:::
