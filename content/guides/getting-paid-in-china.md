Revenue is easy to describe and hard to collect cleanly in China, because the paperwork follows the customer type. A domestic client paying RMB expects a fapiao. A foreign client paying USD expects an invoice it recognises. A platform pays into a merchant account and settles net of fees.

## Three payment routes

| Payer | Currency and channel | Document | Tax point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chinese company | RMB into the basic account | Fapiao, usually special fapiao | VAT 6% (services) or 1%/3% small-scale |
| Foreign company or individual | Foreign currency into a foreign-currency account | Commercial invoice, contract | Exported service, often VAT-exempt with filing |
| Consumer or platform | RMB into a merchant account | Platform settlement, fapiao on request | VAT as above, platform fees are costs |

## Domestic customers and fapiao

The fapiao is both an invoice and a tax document. The buyer deducts input VAT only with a **special** fapiao; an ordinary fapiao supports the expense but not the credit.

- Issue the fapiao through the tax system, matching the contract and the actual service.
- The amount, the buyer's name and credit code, and the service description must match. A mismatched fapiao is a problem for the buyer and a risk for you.
- Do not issue a fapiao before the service is delivered unless you are prepared to recognise the revenue and the VAT.

For a service company, 6% VAT applies as a general taxpayer; a small-scale taxpayer pays 1% through 2027-12-31, or nothing below the monthly threshold.

## Foreign customers: exported services

When the customer is outside China and the service is consumed outside China, the service is generally treated as an exported service and is **VAT-exempt**, not zero-rated with a refund. The practical requirements:

- A contract showing the foreign customer and the service.
- Evidence that the service was consumed abroad.
- A filing or record-keeping step in the tax system for the exemption.

Goods are different: exports of goods can qualify for VAT refunds, which is why trading companies care about exporter status and customs declarations.

Foreign currency arrives in a foreign-currency account and can be converted or kept. The bank will ask for the contract and sometimes the invoice. Keep the contract in English and Chinese if the customer is not a Chinese entity.

:::warn
An "exported service" that is really delivered to a Chinese subsidiary of the foreign customer is not exported. The tax authority looks at who consumes the service and where, not at who pays the invoice. Misclassifying this is a common assessment.
:::

## The withholding you owe as the payer

This is the direction founders forget. When a Chinese company pays a foreign company for services, royalties, interest or dividends, the Chinese payer is the withholding agent:

- **VAT** — usually 6% on services and royalties, withheld and remitted.
- **Corporate income tax** — 10% on gross, unless a tax treaty reduces it (5% for qualifying dividends, royalties sometimes 5–10%, and business profits may be exempt if there is no permanent establishment).
- **Treaty relief** — requires the beneficial owner to claim it, with a certificate of tax residence and substance evidence.

A ¥1m management fee to the parent can therefore cost the Chinese company an extra ~¥160,000 in withheld taxes, payable by the company if it fails to withhold.

## Platform and consumer payments

WeChat Pay and Alipay merchant accounts require the business licence, the basic account and, for online sales, an ICP filing for the website or app. Settlement goes to the company account; the platform fee is a deductible cost with its own fapiao.

Consumers rarely ask for fapiao, but the revenue is still taxable. The platform's settlement report is the record the tax bureau will compare against.

## Getting paid on time

- Price in RMB for domestic contracts; the exchange rate risk is yours otherwise.
- For foreign contracts, agree the currency, the invoicing entity and who bears the transfer fee.
- Milestone payments beat a single final invoice, especially with new customers.
- A Chinese company that has not paid is a collection problem, not a tax problem — the fapiao already created the liability.
- Keep the contract, the invoice and the bank receipt for every payment; the audit will ask for all three.

## A short checklist for the first invoice

:::checklist
- Customer type identified: domestic, foreign, or platform
- Contract signed before the invoice, with the service described concretely
- Buyer's full name and unified credit code correct
- VAT status of the company confirmed before quoting a price
- Export-service exemption filing done before the first foreign receipt
- Withholding computed on any payment to a foreign related party
- Bank receipt retained and matched to the invoice
:::
