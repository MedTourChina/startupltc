Owning a Chinese company does not give you the right to work in it. That requires a work permit, and the permit is tied to the employing entity — which, for a founder, is usually your own WFOE.

This guide is about the ordinary case: a founder with a degree and some experience, working in their own company.

## The permit system in outline

Foreign workers are graded A, B and C:

- **A — high-end talent.** Senior roles, high salary, recognised achievements. Fewer documents, faster processing.
- **B — professional talent.** The category almost all founders use. Generally a bachelor's degree or higher plus two years of relevant work experience, or a salary above the local threshold, or a points score on age, education, salary and experience.
- **C — other.** Limited to quotas and specific situations, such as seasonal or temporary work.

The work permit is issued by the science and technology bureau (foreign experts administration) in most cities, and the residence permit by the exit-entry administration of the public security bureau.

## The sequence

1. **Employer registration.** The company registers on the foreign expert management system.
2. **Work permit notice.** The employer applies for a notification letter, which is what the Z visa is based on.
3. **Z visa.** You apply at a Chinese embassy or consulate abroad with the notification letter, your passport and supporting documents. If you are already in China on another visa, conversion is possible in some cases and not others.
4. **Entry and medical check.**
5. **Work permit card.** After entry, the employer completes the application and you collect the card.
6. **Residence permit.** Apply within 30 days of entry, at the exit-entry bureau, with the work permit and the medical record.
7. **Annual and renewal steps.** The permit is usually valid one to five years, tied to the contract; renewals need the same evidence.

:::warn
Do not work before the permit is issued. Working on a tourist or business visa is a violation that can lead to detention, a fine, deportation and a re-entry ban — and it can jeopardise the company's ability to sponsor permits later.
:::

## The documents that take time

The permit itself is not usually the delay. These are:

- **Degree authentication.** Your degree must be verified — through the Chinese education ministry's service or a consular legalisation/apostille, depending on the city. Allow two to six weeks if you have to obtain documents from your home country.
- **Criminal record check.** From your country of residence, usually within the last six months, legalised or apostilled and translated.
- **Medical examination.** A designated hospital in China, or a medical form completed abroad and verified on arrival.
- **Employment contract.** Signed, with the role, salary and term matching the application.
- **Company documents.** Business licence, seal, legal representative's ID, and evidence the company can pay the salary.

## Salary, tax and the permit

The permit and the payroll are connected. The salary in the contract is what you are expected to be paid, and it is what the tax and social insurance authorities will see. A permit filed with a high salary and a payroll declaring the minimum is a mismatch that shows up in audits.

For category B, many cities set a salary threshold around four times the local average social wage, or accept the degree-plus-experience route instead. The exact figure changes annually and by city.

## Founders' extra problems

- **The company has no revenue yet.** The permit officer may question whether the company can support the salary. A capital contribution, a lease and a business plan help.
- **Your role is "CEO" but the company is one person.** That is normal for a WFOE; explain the business, not the title.
- **Your degree is not in the field.** The two years of experience can compensate, but the evidence has to be documented — letters from employers, not a CV.
- **You changed companies.** A new permit is needed; the old one is cancelled. Do not let the gap exceed the allowed period.

:::checklist
- Degree authenticated and translated
- Criminal record check within validity, legalised
- Medical examination arranged
- Employment contract matching salary and role
- Company able to show capital, lease and business activity
- Application started at least two months before the intended start date
:::

## Family members

Spouses and dependent children can apply for residence permits as dependants, usually tied to the principal permit holder. Dependants cannot work on a dependant permit; a spouse who wants to work needs their own employer and permit — which may be the same company.

## Renewal and permanent residence

Renew before expiry; a gap in status is harder to fix than a renewal. After several years of continuous residence and a clean record, some foreign nationals qualify for permanent residence, with higher thresholds for income and time in China. It is a long path, not a shortcut.

:::note
The permit is a permission to work for a specific employer in a specific role. Changing employer, role or city means a change application. Treat the permit as part of the employment contract, not as a personal document.
:::

Once you can work legally, the next question is who else you hire — and the employment law that comes with them.
