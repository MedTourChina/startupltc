Registering the company takes days. Opening its bank account can take weeks, and it is where a surprising number of foreign-founded companies stall.

The reason is not bureaucracy for its own sake. Chinese banks are subject to strict know-your-customer and anti-money-laundering rules, and a newly registered company with foreign shareholders, no trading history and a virtual address is exactly the profile those rules are designed to slow down.

## The two accounts you will need

Most companies end up with at least two:

- **A basic deposit account (基本户)** — the main RMB account, one per company, used for payroll, tax payments and everyday operations.
- **A capital account (资本金账户)** — a foreign-currency account for capital contributions and, where registered, shareholder loans. Money enters here and is converted under foreign-exchange rules.

Some companies also open a general account for a specific purpose, and exporters may need a foreign-currency settlement account. Start with the basic account and the capital account.

## The documents

Requirements vary by bank and branch, but expect:

- Business licence (original and copy).
- Articles of association.
- Legal representative's ID or passport.
- Shareholder documents, including the full ownership chain up to the ultimate beneficial owners.
- Registered address documents — lease, property certificate, utility bills.
- Company seal, financial seal, legal representative's seal, and the bank U-key application.
- A business description, sometimes a business plan, and the contracts or invoices that show what the company does.
- For foreign shareholders: incorporation documents, often notarised and legalised or apostilled.

:::warn
The ownership chain is where applications fail. If your shareholder is a Hong Kong company owned by a BVI company owned by a trust, the bank will want every layer documented and the ultimate beneficial owner identified. A structure that is easy to draw is easier to bank.
:::

## The process

1. **Choose the bank and the branch.** A branch that has handled foreign-funded companies before is worth more than a marginally better fee schedule.
2. **Pre-screen.** Ask the relationship manager to review the file before you submit. Many branches will tell you informally whether the address and ownership will pass.
3. **Visit in person.** The legal representative usually has to attend, and some banks require a video or in-person interview with the beneficial owner.
4. **Submit and wait.** Two to six weeks is normal; a month is not unusual.
5. **Activate online banking.** The U-key, the passwords and the payment limits are set at activation. Ask what the daily limit is before you need to pay a salary.

## Why applications get rejected

- **The address does not check out.** No signage, no desk, no utility bill, or an address the bank associates with mass registration.
- **The business scope is vague or regulated.** "Consulting" with no contracts, or an activity that needs a licence the company does not hold.
- **The ownership chain is opaque.** Nominee shareholders, bearer shares, or an offshore layer that cannot be verified.
- **Sanctions or high-risk jurisdiction.** A shareholder or director connected to a sanctioned country or a listed entity will trigger enhanced checks, often a refusal.
- **The company looks inactive.** No capital paid in, no staff, no premises, no contracts.
- **The legal representative cannot attend.** Some banks insist on a face-to-face meeting.

A rejection is not usually appealable at the same branch. Applying elsewhere is common, and the second bank will ask why the first refused.

## Making the file strong

- Pay in some capital before applying, and bring the capital-account credit advice.
- Have a lease, a utility bill and a photo of the office with the company name.
- Bring two or three signed contracts or letters of intent with real customers.
- Keep the ownership chain short and documented.
- Make sure the legal representative can attend in person.
- Have a Chinese-speaking colleague or adviser at the meeting.

:::checklist
- Lease, property certificate and utility bill in the company's name
- Full ownership chain with ultimate beneficial owners identified
- Capital paid in and credited to the capital account
- Signed contracts or letters of intent
- Legal representative available to attend
- Bank pre-screened the file before submission
:::

## Fees and limits

Account opening is often free; monthly fees are common (¥10–¥100), and some banks require a minimum balance. International transfers carry fees and a spread. Ask about:

- The daily payment limit and how to raise it.
- The fee for inward and outward remittances.
- Whether the branch supports foreign-currency conversion and cross-border RMB.
- The relationship manager's direct line — you will need it.

## If the bank says no

Alternatives are limited but real: a different bank, a different branch of the same bank, a smaller city commercial bank, or a payment institution for specific collection needs. A corporate account is not optional for a company that employs people or invoices, so treat a refusal as a signal about the file, not the business.

:::note
The account is also where the company's compliance record starts. Late tax filings, unexplained transfers and a dormant account all show up in the bank's ongoing monitoring. A clean first year makes the second account easier.
:::
