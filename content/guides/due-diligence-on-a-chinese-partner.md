Chinese companies are unusually transparent on paper: registry, litigation, enforcement, pledges and penalties are public. The information is there; founders skip it because the partner is friendly and the deal is moving. This is a checklist for the week before signing.

## The registry

Pull the company's record from the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System (国家企业信用信息公示系统, gsxt.gov.cn) and cross-check with a commercial database.

- **Unified social credit code** and exact registered name — use the code, not the brand, in every document.
- **Registered capital vs paid-in capital**: a company with ¥100 million registered and ¥0 paid in is a promise, not a balance sheet. The five-year paid-in rule now forces a schedule; check it.
- **Shareholders and actual controller**: look through holding companies. Nominee arrangements are common and can be undone by the nominee's creditors.
- **Business scope**: does it actually include what they will do for you? A manufacturer whose scope lacks manufacturing is a trading company.
- **Branches, investments, pledges**: equity pledges and asset mortgages tell you what is already committed.
- **Abnormal operations list** (经营异常名录) and serious-violation list: an address-based listing is a warning about the premises and the paperwork.

## Litigation and enforcement

- **Court judgments** (裁判文书网) and hearing notices: contract disputes, labour claims, IP suits.
- **Enforcement** (被执行人) and **dishonest debtor** (失信被执行人) records: an enforcement case means a creditor already tried and failed to collect.
- **Administrative penalties**: tax, customs, market regulation, environment. A pattern matters more than a single fine.
- **Equity freezes** (股权冻结): frozen shares cannot be transferred without the court.

A partner with live enforcement cases, repeated penalties in the same area, or frozen equity is not a diligence problem — it is a stop.

## The licences

For each licence the partner claims, get the certificate, check the holder's name matches, check the validity date, and check the scope. Licences are not transferable, and a partner's licence does not become yours by signing a JV contract.

- Sector licences (food, medical devices, transport, travel agency).
- Customs registration and AEO status for importers.
- High-tech enterprise status for the tax rate — and its expiry.
- Land use rights and property certificates for any site.

## The site

Visit. Then check:

- The address on the lease matches the registry address.
- The 房产证 (property certificate) holder is the landlord, or the sublease is consented to in writing.
- Production is actually happening: machines running, shifts working, inventory moving.
- Environmental and fire approvals cover the actual activity, not just the building.
- Staff count on the social insurance record is consistent with the size of the operation.

## The people

- Legal representative, directors and supervisors: who are they, and do they appear in other companies with problems?
- The person you are negotiating with: are they a shareholder, a director, or an employee with no authority? Ask for the ID and the authorisation.
- Key-person dependency: if the deal depends on one manager, put a non-compete and a handover obligation in the contract.

## Financial diligence

- Audited financial statements for three years, plus the tax returns that match them.
- Bank statements for the last twelve months, not just a letter from the bank.
- Accounts receivable ageing and the top ten customers; call two of them.
- Related-party transactions: sales to the shareholder's other company at off-market prices are a value leak.
- Inventory: count it or have it counted; the number in the books is a claim.

## Turning findings into contract terms

| Finding | Contract response |
|---|---|
| Capital not paid in | Contribution schedule with penalties; escrow until paid |
| Licence critical to the deal | Condition precedent; termination right if not maintained |
| Enforcement cases | Escrow of the purchase price until cleared |
| IP held by the founder personally | Assignment as a condition of closing |
| Related-party dependence | Representations, disclosure schedule, indemnity |
| Landlord is not the owner | Consent to sublease, or walk |

## Five red flags that end the conversation

1. The registered address is a virtual address shared with hundreds of companies.
2. Paid-in capital is zero and the shareholder refuses a schedule.
3. The licence is held by a different company than the one you are contracting with.
4. The person signing cannot produce a sealed authorisation.
5. The financial statements do not match the tax filings.

:::note
Public records are evidence, not proof. A clean record can still hide a bad operator; a bad record is rarely wrong. Use the records to decide where to dig, and a site visit to decide whether to sign.
:::
