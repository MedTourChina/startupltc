Founders usually ask "how long does registration take?" Registration is the fast part. The 90 days that matter are the decisions made before it and the banking, tax and hiring that follow.

## Weeks 0–2: decisions that change everything downstream

| Decision | Why it is first | What it rules out |
|---|---|---|
| Entity or no entity | An importer, a licensor or a platform seller may not need a Chinese company | Saves ¥15,000–40,000 a year |
| Sector access | The negative list and the sector licence decide whether you can own it | A lease you cannot licence |
| City | Costs, permits and hiring differ; the founder's residence permit is tied to the city | A company in a city where you do not live |
| Structure | WFOE, JV, or a Hong Kong holdco above either | A restructuring within a year |

Do not file anything until these four are settled. Filing a name is cheap; unwinding a company is not.

## Weeks 2–4: filings that run in parallel

- **Name self-declaration** (自主申报) — same-day in most cities, valid for a short window.
- **Registered address** — lease signed, property certificate and landlord consent in hand. Do not sign before the district accepts the address for your business scope.
- **Trademark** — file the Latin name, the Chinese name and the logo in the relevant classes now. Nine to twelve months later, you either own them or you are negotiating with someone who does.
- **Hong Kong holdco**, if you want one — incorporation and bank account take longer than the mainland company; start early.
- **Articles of association** — decide governance, reserved matters and the legal representative before the template is filled in.

## Weeks 3–6: incorporation

1. Business licence (营业执照) with the unified social credit code.
2. Seal carving and filing with the public security bureau.
3. Bank account opening — the slowest step for foreign-invested companies; expect 2–6 weeks and a compliance interview.
4. Tax registration and the choice between small-scale and general taxpayer.
5. Foreign exchange registration for the capital account, usually at the bank.
6. Social insurance and housing fund accounts, even before the first hire.

Typical elapsed time from name to licence: one to three weeks. From licence to a bank account that can receive foreign capital: two to six weeks. These overlap only if you push the bank appointment the day the licence is issued.

## Weeks 5–10: the operating setup

- **Premises**: fit-out, fire, and any premises licence (food, health, special industry). This is the longest lead time in restaurants, clinics and hotels.
- **Accounting**: a bookkeeping firm from month one, not from the first tax filing.
- **Payments**: a corporate card, a payment gateway, or a licensed payment institution for cross-border collection.
- **Hiring**: work permit and residence permit for the foreign founder; contracts and social insurance for local staff.
- **Website and ICP**: filing takes 3–6 weeks after the domain and hosting are ready.

## Weeks 8–12: first revenue

- **Invoicing**: fapiao issuance requires the tax bureau's approval of the invoicing system and the correct business scope.
- **First VAT filing**: monthly or quarterly depending on taxpayer status.
- **Contracts**: Chinese-language versions with the company seal; foreign-language versions for your own records.
- **Annual compliance calendar**: put the dates in the diary now — annual report by 30 June, CIT reconciliation by 31 May.

## Where the plan actually breaks

1. **The bank account.** KYC on a foreign-owned company is slow; a founder who assumed one week loses a month.
2. **The address.** A virtual or shared address passes the registry and fails the bank or the sector licence.
3. **The licence.** Food, medical, education and travel licences take months and depend on the premises, not on the company.
4. **The founder's own permit.** You cannot legally work in your own company without a work permit; the clock starts after incorporation.
5. **The five-year capital rule.** The contribution schedule is in the articles; an unrealistic promise becomes a liability.

## A realistic timeline

| Day | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 0 | Decisions: entity, sector, city, structure |
| 14 | Name reserved, address signed, trademark filed |
| 30 | Business licence issued, seals carved |
| 45 | Bank account opening in progress, tax registration done |
| 60 | Capital account registered, first funds in |
| 75 | Premises licensed (if needed), first hires onboarded |
| 90 | First invoice issued, filing calendar set |

Any of these can move; the ones that move most are the bank, the premises licence and the founder's permit. Plan the business around 120 days to first revenue, and treat 90 as the good case.

:::checklist
- Entity decision written down, with the annual cost
- Sector access confirmed in writing by counsel or the regulator
- City chosen for cost, talent and permit reasons
- Name reserved; address accepted by the district
- Trademark filed in Latin, Chinese and logo
- Bank appointment booked the day the licence is issued
- Work permit application started
- Bookkeeping engaged before the first filing
:::
