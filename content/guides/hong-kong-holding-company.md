The Hong Kong holding structure is the most common piece of advice given to foreign founders in China. It is sometimes right, often premature, and occasionally harmful. This guide separates the three questions that get conflated: Chinese tax on profits, Hong Kong tax on the holding company, and the exit.

## The structure

A typical arrangement is:

- A Hong Kong company owns 100% of the mainland WFOE.
- The WFOE earns profits, pays Chinese corporate income tax, and distributes dividends to the Hong Kong parent.
- The Hong Kong parent either reinvests, lends to the WFOE, or distributes to its own shareholders.

The mainland-to-Hong Kong dividend is the point of the structure.

## The withholding tax math

China taxes dividends paid by a Chinese company to a non-resident shareholder at **10%** by default. Under the mainland–Hong Kong double taxation arrangement, the rate can fall to **5%** where the Hong Kong recipient holds at least **25%** of the Chinese company and is the beneficial owner.

On a ¥1,000,000 dividend, that is ¥50,000 instead of ¥100,000 — a real saving, but only when profits are actually distributed.

To claim the 5% rate the Hong Kong company needs, among other things:

- A **certificate of residence** from the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department.
- Evidence of **substance**: an office, staff, decision-making in Hong Kong, and accounts.
- Beneficial ownership — not a shell that immediately passes the dividend to a third jurisdiction.

Chinese tax authorities have become considerably stricter about all three. A newly formed shell with a nominee director is unlikely to qualify.

:::warn
The 5% rate is a treaty benefit, not an automatic entitlement. If the Hong Kong company is a conduit, the authorities can deny the benefit and assess the difference. Build the substance before you need the certificate, not after.
:::

## Hong Kong tax on the holding company

Hong Kong taxes profits arising in Hong Kong. A holding company that only receives dividends and interest may claim the income is offshore and not taxable — but since the 2023 reform of the foreign-sourced income exemption regime, passive income needs economic substance in Hong Kong to remain non-taxable.

In practice this means the Hong Kong company needs:

- A real office or a serviced office address that is not a mailbox.
- At least one local director or staff member with decision-making authority.
- Board meetings and minutes in Hong Kong.
- Annual audit and profits tax filing.

A compliant small holding company costs roughly HK$15,000–HK$40,000 a year to maintain, including company secretary, audit and tax filing. That is the price of the treaty benefit.

## What the structure does not do

- **It does not reduce Chinese corporate income tax.** The WFOE still pays 25% (or the small-company effective 5%).
- **It does not avoid Chinese withholding tax on royalties or interest.** Those have their own rates and treaty conditions.
- **It does not defer Chinese tax indefinitely.** China's controlled foreign corporation rules can attribute profits of a low-taxed offshore company to a Chinese resident shareholder.
- **It does not automatically help an exit.** Selling Hong Kong shares can trigger Chinese indirect-transfer rules.

## The exit question

The usual reason to hold through Hong Kong is a future sale or listing. Two things matter:

1. **Indirect transfer.** China taxes gains on the transfer of shares in an offshore company that holds Chinese assets, where the transfer has no reasonable commercial purpose. A buyer of your Hong Kong shares may require an indemnity, or the deal may be structured to avoid the issue.
2. **Listing.** Hong Kong and offshore listing structures are common, and a Hong Kong holdco sits naturally in them. If an IPO is a realistic five-year plan, the structure is easier to build now than to insert later.

If neither applies, the holding company is an option you are paying for annually.

## When to set it up

Set up the Hong Kong company when at least two of these are true:

- You expect to distribute profits within the next three years.
- You are raising from investors who require an offshore holding structure.
- You plan to sell the business or list it.
- You will hold IP or contracts outside China.
- You have, or will hire, genuine Hong Kong substance.

If only one is true, or none, a direct foreign shareholder is simpler, cheaper and easier to explain to a bank.

:::checklist
- Is there a real Hong Kong office and a local decision-maker?
- Will the company obtain a certificate of residence?
- Is the 5% saving larger than the annual maintenance cost?
- Has the exit structure been checked for indirect-transfer exposure?
- Has a Chinese tax adviser reviewed the CFC question?
:::

## A note on cost

The Hong Kong company itself is not expensive to incorporate — a few thousand Hong Kong dollars. The recurring cost is the audit, the company secretary and the substance. Founders routinely budget for incorporation and forget the annual bill.

:::rule
Build the holding structure for a transaction you can name — a dividend, a round, a sale — not for the idea that it might be useful one day.
:::
