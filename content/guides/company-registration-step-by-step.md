Most guides describe registration as "submit documents, get a licence". In practice the registry is the fast part. The weeks disappear in notarisation, translation, address verification and the bank's compliance review.

This is the sequence that works, with realistic timings for a wholly foreign-owned company in a tier-one or tier-two city.

## The eight steps

| Step | What happens | Realistic time |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Name | Self-declaration through the local one-stop system | 1–3 working days |
| 2. Documents | Shareholder papers apostilled, translated, signed | 2–6 weeks (mostly outside China) |
| 3. Submission | Articles, application form, address and investor documents filed with the market regulator | 3–10 working days |
| 4. Licence and seals | Business licence issued; five seals carved and recorded | 1–3 working days |
| 5. Bank account | Basic RMB account and capital account opened | 2–6 weeks |
| 6. Tax | Tax bureau registration, e-tax account, invoice application | 3–10 working days |
| 7. FX and people | Foreign-exchange registration through the bank, social insurance and housing fund | 1–2 weeks |
| 8. Sector licence | Only if the activity is on the restricted list | 2 weeks to 6 months |

Steps 6 and 7 can often run in parallel with step 5, but the bank usually needs the tax registration before it will activate the account.

## Step 1: the name

The name has four parts: administrative division, trade name (字号), industry, and company form. You declare it yourself through the local system; there is no approval meeting for an ordinary name.

Two checks matter more than the registry:

- The trade name must not conflict with an existing registration in the same industry and jurisdiction.
- It must not conflict with a registered trademark, and it must not use words that suggest a state body, an international organisation or a misleading claim.

Check the trademark register before you fall in love with a name. A name that clears the company registry and infringes a trademark is a letter from a law firm waiting to happen.

:::note
The trade name is not a trademark. Registering the company gives you no exclusive right to the words. If the name matters, file the trademark in the same month.
:::

## Step 2: the documents that actually take time

For a foreign corporate shareholder, expect:

- Certificate of incorporation, current and complete.
- Register of members or equivalent ownership evidence, showing the chain up to the ultimate beneficial owner.
- Board resolution authorising the investment and naming the signatory.
- Passport copy of the legal representative.
- All of the above apostilled (for Hague Convention members) or consular-legalised, then translated into Chinese by a translation company the registry accepts.

The apostille must be recent. Several registries reject documents older than six months, and a certificate with a missing page or an unstamped register is the single most common cause of a rejected filing.

For an individual shareholder: passport, and a notarised or apostilled copy depending on the registry. Some cities accept a signed declaration in person.

## Step 3: articles and submission

The articles of association are short for a simple company: name, address, scope, registered capital, contributions, governance, profit distribution, dissolution. Chinese law allows considerably more flexibility than the older template suggests, including a single director, a supervisor replaced by an audit committee in some structures, and non-proportional voting if the articles say so.

The registered capital and the five-year paid-in rule matter here. Commit an amount you can actually pay within five years, and put the schedule in the articles.

## Step 4: licence and seals

The business licence carries the unified social credit code, which is the company's identity for tax, banking and everything else. Then come the seals:

- Company seal (公章) — the general corporate seal.
- Financial seal (财务专用章) — banks and tax.
- Legal representative's seal (法人章) — cheques and some filings.
- Contract seal (合同专用章) — optional.
- Invoice seal (发票专用章) — for fapiao.

Budget ¥200–800 for carving and recording. Keep a seal register from day one.

## Step 5: the bank

This is the longest and least predictable step. A new foreign-owned company with no history is a high-risk profile for the bank's KYC team, and a virtual address or an unclear business story will stall the file.

Bring: licence, articles, all seals, legal representative's ID, address lease and property certificate, and the ownership chain. Expect a visit, an interview and two to six weeks.

:::warn
Do not treat the bank as a formality after registration. If the account is refused, the company can hold a licence, owe tax filings and still be unable to pay a salary. Line up the bank before you file, and ask the relationship manager to pre-screen the address and ownership chain.
:::

## Step 6: tax registration

Register with the tax authority, set up the electronic tax account, and decide the taxpayer status:

- **Small-scale taxpayer** — 1% VAT on turnover (through 2027-12-31), simpler filing.
- **General taxpayer** — 13%/9%/6% with input credit, required above ¥5m of annual sales or by choice.

Choose deliberately. A company that will sell to other companies and needs to issue 6% or 13% special fapiao generally wants general-taxpayer status early; a small consultancy billing individuals usually does not.

## Step 7: foreign exchange and people

The foreign-exchange registration is normally filed through the bank when the capital account is opened. Capital contributions then arrive in the capital account and are converted under the bank's review.

Register for social insurance and the housing fund before the first payroll. Both are legally required from the first employee, including the foreign founder if employed.

## Step 8: sector licences

If the business is on the restricted list — education, healthcare, certain food and logistics activities, value-added telecom — the business licence is only the beginning. The licence application runs after registration and can take weeks or months, and some licences require the premises to be fitted out and inspected first.

## What it costs

| Item | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Government fees | Under ¥1,000 |
| Agent or filing service | ¥3,000–10,000 |
| Translation and apostille | ¥1,000–5,000 |
| Seals | ¥200–800 |
| Address (first year) | ¥3,000–25,000 |
| Bank account | Free to ¥2,000 |
| Total, simple company | ¥8,000–35,000 |

## The five stalls

1. **Apostille rejected** — stale, incomplete or untranslated shareholder documents.
2. **Address rejected** — no lease, no property certificate, no consent to sublease, or an address the bank treats as mass registration.
3. **Name conflict** — registry or trademark.
4. **Scope needs a licence** — filed as a general activity when it is a licensed one.
5. **Legal representative abroad** — some banks and registries require physical presence at signing or activation.

:::checklist
- Name checked against the trademark register, not just the company register
- Shareholder documents apostilled within the last six months and translated
- Registered capital you can pay within five years, schedule written into the articles
- Lease, property certificate and sublease consent in hand before filing
- Bank branch pre-screened, with a named relationship manager
- Taxpayer status chosen deliberately before the first invoice
- Seal register opened the day the seals are carved
:::
