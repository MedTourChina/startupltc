Premises are the second-largest fixed cost after payroll, and the one most often chosen badly. Founders optimise for rent per square metre and then discover the address cannot be registered, the landlord cannot issue a fapiao, or the building's use permit does not cover their business.

The lease is a compliance document as much as a commercial one.

## The options

| Option | Typical cost | Registration | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Co-working desk | ¥1,500–3,500 per desk per month | Usually yes, via the operator's address pool | Solo founder, first months |
| Serviced office | ¥2,500–6,000 per desk per month | Yes, operator provides registration and mail | Small team, client meetings |
| Private office (Grade B) | ¥4–8 per m² per day | Yes, with property certificate and lease | Team of 5–30 |
| Private office (Grade A) | ¥8–15 per m² per day | Yes | Client-facing, central location |
| Industrial park / incubator | ¥1–4 per m² per day, plus subsidies | Yes, park handles filing | Manufacturing-adjacent, grant-seeking |

These are market observations for late 2026, before management fees and tax. Shanghai and Beijing sit at the top; Chengdu, Wuhan and Xi'an are materially cheaper.

## What the registry needs

To use an address for company registration, the registry generally wants:

- A lease in the company's name (or a pre-incorporation lease in the founder's name, assignable).
- The landlord's property ownership certificate (房产证) or an equivalent title document.
- A filed lease (租赁备案) in cities that require it.
- A premises use certificate (场地使用证明) in some districts.
- For subleases, the head lease and the landlord's written consent.

The address must also be a real, identifiable place: the bank will visit it, and the market regulator may too.

:::warn
A "virtual address" sold for registration only is the most common cause of an abnormal-operations listing. The registry checks, the bank checks, and the tax bureau checks. If nobody can find your company at the address on the licence, the listing follows — and it blocks banking, invoicing and the legal representative's travel.
:::

## The lease terms that matter

| Clause | What to check | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Term | 1–3 years is normal; match to your funding runway | A 3-year lease is a fixed cost you cannot exit |
| Deposit | 2–3 months is standard | Negotiate a smaller deposit or a staged one |
| Rent-free period | 30–90 days for fit-out is common | Fit-out takes longer than you think |
| Rent escalation | 3–8% per year, or fixed | Model the full term, not year one |
| Management fee | ¥15–40 per m² per month, often separate | Can add 20–30% to the rent |
| Break clause | Exit after 12–24 months with 2–3 months' notice | Essential for a startup |
| Assignment / sublease | Allowed with consent, or not | Needed if you shrink or move |
| Fit-out approval | Landlord's approval and building rules | Fire and electrical work needs permits |
| Restoration | Return in original condition or pay | Can be a large end-of-term cost |
| Registration | Landlord warrants the address can be registered | The whole point |

A lease without a break clause is a bet that the business will not change for the full term. Most businesses change faster.

## The tax side of rent

Rent is deductible for corporate income tax only with a valid **rent fapiao** issued by the landlord in the company's name.

- VAT on rent is **9%** for a general-taxpayer landlord, or **5%** under simplified rules for older buildings. A landlord who cannot issue a fapiao — often an individual or a small landlord — is effectively 9% more expensive.
- Stamp duty on the lease is **0.1%** of the total rent, paid by both parties; small-scale taxpayers pay half.
- The landlord bears property tax (12% of rent, or 1.2% of the property's residual value if self-used), which is usually priced into the rent.

:::note
Ask for a sample fapiao before signing. A landlord who says "we don't issue invoices, but the rent is lower" is offering a discount that costs you the deduction and creates a bookkeeping problem at year end.
:::

## Fit-out and permits

Interior construction needs the building's approval and, for larger works, a fire-safety filing. In Shanghai and Beijing, a small office fit-out can be done in two to four weeks; a restaurant or clinic fit-out is a different project with its own inspections.

Budget 20–40% of the first year's rent for fit-out if the space is bare, and check whether the landlord requires restoration at the end.

## Negotiation levers

- **Longer term, lower rent.** A two-year term with a break clause can get 5–10% off.
- **Rent-free fit-out.** Easier to win than a rent reduction, and worth the same in cash.
- **Management fee cap.** Ask for a fixed management fee for the term.
- **Deposit staged.** Pay half on signing and half on handover.
- **Fapiao commitment.** Put the invoicing obligation in the contract, with a remedy if it is not met.

## The lease checklist

:::checklist
- Property ownership certificate seen and matched to the landlord's ID or licence
- Address confirmed as registrable by the registry or a local agent, in writing
- Landlord's fapiao obligation written into the contract
- Lease term and break clause modelled against the funding runway
- Management fee, utilities and restoration costs identified
- Fit-out approval path and fire filing understood
- Stamp duty budgeted
- Lease filed (备案) where the city requires it
- Bank pre-notified of the address and a visit arranged
:::

:::rule
Cheap premises are cheap for a reason: no fapiao, no registration, or a landlord who disappears. Pay for the address that satisfies all three audiences — registry, bank and inspector — and put the fapiao obligation in the contract.
:::
