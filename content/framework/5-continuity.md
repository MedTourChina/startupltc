Registration ends; compliance begins. The fifth layer is about the obligations that do not stop: the permit you need to work in your own company, the payroll you must run correctly, and the filings that decide whether the company stays in good standing.

## Working in your own company

A foreign founder cannot simply own a Chinese company and work in it. To work — to manage, sell, hire, sign — you need a **work permit** sponsored by the company, and a residence permit tied to it.

The permit system grades applicants:

- **Category A** — high-end talent: senior scientists, entrepreneurs with a substantial investment or a recognised track record, and some highly paid executives. Fewer documents, longer validity.
- **Category B** — professionals: a bachelor's degree or higher, two years of relevant experience, and a salary above the local threshold. This is where most founders land.
- **Category C** — temporary, seasonal or quota-limited work. Narrow and rarely useful for a founder.

The employer must be registered, taxed and able to show a payroll. A company with no revenue and no salary payments can still sponsor a permit initially, but renewals get harder — which is why founders should pay themselves a salary, however modest, from the start.

:::note
The permit is tied to the employer, not to the person. If you change companies, the permit must be transferred or re-applied for. If the company closes, the permit ends with it, and your residence permit follows.
:::

## Payroll and the real cost of a hire

Every employee needs a written employment contract, enrolment in social insurance and, in most cities, the housing provident fund. Employer contributions commonly add 25–40% to gross salary, depending on the city and the contribution base.

Beyond the statutory cost, two Chinese labour-law features matter:

- **Severance.** Dismissal without a statutory ground, or without the right process, costs two months' salary per year of service; lawful dismissal costs one month per year. Probation does not remove the requirement for a reason.
- **Fixed-term renewals.** After two consecutive fixed-term contracts, the employee can generally require an open-ended contract. Plan contract lengths deliberately.

The [first-hire guide](/guides/hiring-first-employee-china/) covers contracts, probation, social insurance and severance in detail.

## The annual calendar

A Chinese company has a recurring filing rhythm. The dates are national; the portals are local.

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Monthly or quarterly | VAT, surcharges, individual income tax withholding, social insurance |
| January–June | Market-regulator annual report (due 30 June) |
| January–June | Foreign-exchange annual declaration, where applicable |
| 31 May | Corporate income tax annual settlement |
| By 30 June | Statistics filings, where applicable |
| Annually | Statutory audit for foreign-invested companies |

Missing the annual report puts the company on the **abnormal operations list**, which can freeze bank account changes, block participation in tenders and trigger fines. The [compliance calendar](/guides/annual-compliance-calendar/) lists every item by month.

## Changes, not just filings

A company is not static. Every material change has a registration or filing consequence: a new shareholder, a capital increase, a change of address, a change of legal representative, a new business scope, a branch. Each requires a resolution, an amended article where relevant, and a registration change — and some require tax clearance first.

Founders often discover this when they try to open a new bank account with an old address on the licence, or when an investor's due diligence finds a shareholder who was never registered.

## The exit

Eventually the company closes, and closure is a process, not a form. Liquidation comes first: creditors, employees, tax, assets. Then tax clearance, then deregistration of the licence, then the bank accounts and seals.

A clean company with no disputes takes six to twelve months. A company with unpaid tax, a departed shareholder or a lost seal takes longer and costs more. Abandoning the company does not close it; the entity keeps accruing filings and penalties, and the legal representative's record follows them.

:::kill
If you are planning to leave China within a year, do not register a company unless you have budgeted the closure — money, time and a representative who will sign. An abandoned company is the most expensive souvenir in the country.
:::

## The continuity test

A company is sustainable when these are true:

- You have a work permit, and a salary that supports its renewal.
- Every employee has a contract, social insurance and a payroll record.
- The annual filings have an owner — you, a finance manager or a firm — with dates in a calendar.
- The address is stable, documented and can survive an inspection.
- There is a written plan for closure, including who will sign and what it will cost.

If any of these is missing, the company is not yet a company; it is a registration with an expiry date.

:::checklist
- Work permit application started before you begin working.
- Salary set to satisfy Category B and to create a tax record.
- Payroll, social insurance and housing fund set up before the first hire.
- Annual filing calendar owned by a named person or firm.
- Closure budget and a willing signatory identified at the start.
:::
