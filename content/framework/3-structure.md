Structure is where founders overthink and underread. Overthink, because the menu of vehicles is short; underread, because the documents that actually govern the company — the articles of association, the joint-venture contract, the seal custody arrangement — are decided at registration and are expensive to change.

## The four vehicles

**Wholly foreign-owned enterprise (WFOE).** A limited liability company owned 100% by a foreign investor. It can trade, invoice, hire, hold licences and own assets. It is the default for service, trading, software and most manufacturing businesses. The foreign investor can be an individual or a company, onshore or offshore.

**Equity joint venture (JV).** A company owned by a foreign investor and a Chinese partner, with the split, the board and the profit distribution set in a joint-venture contract. Required where the negative list restricts foreign equity; chosen voluntarily where the partner brings a licence, land, a customer base or government access.

**Representative office (RO).** A liaison office of a foreign company. It can do market research, coordinate suppliers and represent the parent, but it cannot sign business contracts, invoice or receive revenue in its own name. It is cheap to run and narrow in use.

**Hong Kong (or offshore) holding company.** Not an operating vehicle. It sits above the mainland entity and holds the shares. Its value is in financing, currency, exit and — sometimes — a lower withholding tax on dividends. Its cost is an extra audit, registered office, annual filings and a bank's worth of due diligence.

## What actually differs

Founders compare tax rates first. They should compare control, liability, exit and cost of maintenance, in that order.

| | WFOE | JV | RO | HK holdco |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ownership | 100% foreign | Shared, per licence | None | 100% of the mainland entity |
| Control | Full, subject to articles | Board and vetoes | Parent | Indirect |
| Trading | Yes | Yes | No | Through the WFOE |
| Liability | Limited to capital | Limited to capital | Parent exposed | Limited |
| Tax | CIT 25% (5% small) | CIT at company level | Cost centre | Offshore profits, HK rules |
| Setup time | 3–8 weeks | 3–9 months | 4–8 weeks | 1–3 weeks |
| Annual cost | Address, books, audit | Plus partner governance | Low | Audit, secretary, filings |
| Exit | Transfer or liquidate | Partner consent | Close | Sell or liquidate |

The table hides the important part: **a JV is a marriage, and the articles are the prenuptial agreement.** Deadlock, dilution, related-party pricing, board seats, dividend policy and the right to buy the other side out are all decided when you have the least information and the most optimism.

:::warn
A 51/49 split does not give you control if the board is 2–2, the legal representative is your partner, the seal is in their office and the bank token is with their finance manager. Control is a bundle of specific rights, not a percentage.
:::

## The control bundle

If you take one thing from this layer, take this list. Whoever holds these holds the company:

1. **Board composition and voting.** Simple majority or unanimity, and who chairs.
2. **Legal representative.** Signs for the company; personally accountable; can be changed, but not quietly.
3. **The seal (公章).** Physically executes documents. Its custody is a governance question, not an admin one.
4. **The bank token and the finance login.** Whoever can move money can move money.
5. **The tax login and the fapiao system.** Access to the invoice system is access to revenue.
6. **The shareholder register and the articles.** The registry's record is the legal truth.

A founder who owns 100% but has given the seal and the bank token to a local manager has delegated control. That can be the right decision — but it should be a decision, documented, with a way to revoke it.

## The Hong Kong question

The Hong Kong holding company is the most over-sold structure in the market. It is genuinely useful when:

- You plan to raise offshore capital and want a clean cap table above the mainland entity.
- You want to sell the business one day and prefer to sell shares offshore.
- You need a currency and treasury layer outside the capital-account rules.
- Your investors require it.

It is decoration when it exists only because a consultant recommended it, has no substance, and adds an audit and a tax filing to a company that has not yet traded. The treaty benefit on dividends requires substance and documentation; a shell company with a nominee director is not automatically entitled to it.

## Choosing in practice

The decision tree is short:

- Do you need to invoice or hire in China? If no, do not register an operating company.
- Does the sector require a Chinese partner? If yes, a JV, and spend your money on the contract.
- Do you need to control the business and hold the licence? A WFOE.
- Do you need presence but not trade? An RO, if the parent is content with a cost centre.
- Do you need an offshore ownership layer for financing or exit? Add a Hong Kong company above the WFOE.

Everything else is detail — important detail, but detail that follows from these answers.

:::rule
Choose the simplest structure that does the job today, and make it easy to add a layer later. Founders who build a four-company structure for a business with no revenue spend their first year maintaining it.
:::

The next layer is money: how much capital to promise, how it arrives, and how it leaves.
